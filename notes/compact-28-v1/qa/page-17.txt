7.6
History-cap outcomes and deployment costs
The history cap’s fixed-peer theorem does not imply a better joint outcome after sellers recompute
their reports. The 432-cell recomputation kept the original response-search budget, public scores
and rule weights. Its multiobjective result is consequential: attacked MAE increased in 94 cells
and failed-query rates in 145, despite unchanged honest MAE. Figure 8 shows those costs alongside
inflation reductions. Complete per-rule counts and conditional intervals are indexed in Appendix E.
Thirty-three capped cells retained at least one nonconverged batch and maximum remaining
regret was 0.204423 log units. No capped result is therefore described as a certified market equi-
librium. The descriptive 10% inflation / 5% honest-error gate does not constrain attacked error
or failed queries; threshold sensitivity cannot turn a single-metric improvement into Pareto domi-
nance. These costs also motivate the final-record alternative and its natural-missingness boundary
in Section 5.
7.7
Applicability beyond the zero-cost capped design
Positive concealment costs changed some rankings through deterrence. In the synthetic top-B sub-
set, stipulated costs near 0.02 sharply reduced concealment and moved outcomes toward ordinary
valuation; feedback no longer worsened the mean inflation in the high-cost specifications. Yet over
94% of attempted queries failed in the uniform-cost subset, residual concealment remained positive,
and the real-source directions stayed adverse. This supports a conditional regime change rather
than a general feedback remedy. The full 108-cell cost design and net-deviation bounds remain in
Appendices E and B.
A separate frozen-original-model check removed the real-source evaluation caps, covering all
1,374 JUCars and 10,672 MUCars validation records. All 24 feedback point directions remained
adverse. The two MUCars masking/suppression intervals still crossed zero, with fitting-seed vari-
ability dominating the uncapped conditional variance. The cohort and record-count weighting both
changed, so this is not a pure sample-size experiment or evidence of zero effect. It does not extend
the RPL evaluation population: the RPL ten-seed study retains its fixed 1,000 listings per source.
Complete uncapped counts, weighting and variance decompositions are in Appendices E and B.
