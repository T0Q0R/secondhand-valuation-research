7.4
Controlled components and a search-strength intervention
The complete suppression-score/feedback/gap factorial retained all eight combinations, including
removal of two or all three switched components (Appendix D). These switches are experimental
components, not three independently established innovations. Gap removal substantially increased
mean primary inflation on the real sources, whereas the full three-component configuration was not
uniformly best.
The original scoring network was refreshed each epoch, rather than frozen throughout training.
We crossed epoch versus minibatch score refresh with weak (two sweeps, one start) versus strong
(20 sweeps, four starts) training-response search. Both retained two six-epoch rounds and matched
no-feedback references. Figure 7 shows the audited paired improvements and conditional intervals
at 10% capacity, retaining three fitting seeds. On synthetic, epoch-weak inflation was 0.02075,
epoch-strong 0.01279, batch-strong 0.01269, and no-feedback 0.01303. Thus strengthening search
substantially reduced the weak implementation’s damage, and the strong means were slightly below
the no-feedback reference. Strong JUCars means were also slightly lower than no-feedback, whereas
all four MUCars feedback means remained higher. Changing score-refresh frequency alone was not
a consistent remedy. These are conditional mean comparisons, not proof of a uniquely identified
cause or population-wide feedback benefit.
