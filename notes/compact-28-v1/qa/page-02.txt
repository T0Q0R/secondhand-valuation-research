We link recovery-path diagnosis to a targeted training intervention. First, an information fron-
tier isolates the limits imposed by uniform honest precision and observationally identical missing
reports.
Its restricted family does not predict a learned model’s empirical optimum.
Second,
a signed recovery decomposition exposes gain-increasing intermediate records in fitted four-field
models. Recovery-path regularization (RPL) penalizes those increases without adding private de-
ployment inputs. Third, matched experiments separate initial objective, acquisition, feedback and
allocation, then assess RPL with ten fitting seeds, component removals, budget variation and four-
stage computational checks.
The evidence supports conditional use.
At the declared unit-weight endpoint, RPL lowers
inflation in all thirty source–seed comparisons, but synthetic honest error and query failures rise.
Stronger training-response search removes some earlier feedback damage, restricting that negative
finding to its implementation. Competitive simple controls, finite-response regret and cross-machine
numerical differences further bound the claims. The practical question is therefore which recovery-
aware intervention improves the joint incentive, prediction and verification outcome for a stated
setting.

Related work
Withholding and verification.
Support-margin imputation addresses strategic missingness in
regression [9]; truthful classifiers address withholding in classification [5]. Our precision frontier
adds a uniform honest-quote constraint to a hard query budget, rather than introducing ambiguous
missingness as a new problem. Audit and partial-verification mechanisms [4, 6] motivate checking
reports, while established marginal representations [3] permit compact finite allocation.
Their
guarantees remain specific to their mechanism classes.
Acquisition and strategic feedback.
ACO [12], amortized greedy selection (GDFS) [17],
discriminative information estimation (DIME) [18], stochastic latent encodings (SEFA) [19] and
explanation-ranked acquisition [20] broaden the acquisition comparison beyond risk and predictive
benefit. We run task adapters, with boundaries in Appendix C; they are not oﬀicial end-to-end
reproductions. Performative prediction and strategic risk minimization [10, 11] study deployment-
dependent responses; contextual games and audit-risk design [7, 8] condition decisions on available
information. We neither transfer their convergence guarantees to our finite nonconvex loop nor
claim randomization or monotonicity as new principles. The contribution is the recovery-targeted
loss and its controlled evidence, including search-strength interventions that qualify feedback fail-
ure.
