qcap(s, a) ≤q(s, a) while qcap(H, a) = qH. Subtracting qH, averaging and maximizing over legal
own reports proves that the worst fixed-peer gain weakly decreases. This is not a guarantee after
peers recompute responses, nor a guarantee of lower absolute error.
The cap can quote identical final records differently because it retains history, and lies outside
the verification-neutral frontier class.
An alternative is q↓(r) = minz∈C(r) q(z) on a fixed com-
pletion domain. If completion sets are nonempty and nested and cover the truthful record, then
C(rH) ⊆C(r) implies q↓(r) ≤q↓(rH), eliminating gains over its own honest quote with nonnegative
costs. Unlike the history cap, it can reduce honest quotes and worsen MAE. Unsupported records
need abstention or an uncertified fallback. This envelope is related to established truthful with-
holding prediction [5, 9]; a restricted empirical cost pilot is reported in Appendix B. Equal-record
consistency, appeals and possible selection disparities require explicit platform review; we have not
measured protected-group effects or deployment fairness.

The original factorial crosses two initial objectives, three acquisition scores, feedback on/off and
two allocators: 24 cells per source/seed/capacity. Sharing no-feedback fits gives fourteen unique
fits. Every outer fold is excluded from its entire fitting loop; the remaining records are split into

initial epochs. Models are then cloned into one no-feedback and six score/allocator-specific fits,
with two three-epoch robust updates and identical minibatch orders. Guidance heads refresh after
each round. At each feedback epoch’s start, public scores are fixed; within minibatches the current
detached predictor searches responses with one start and two sweeps at 10% capacity. No-feedback
uses no-query response search with the same update budget. Deployment heads use assembled
excluded-fold targets and deployment encodings; the final predictor is not updated after that head
fit. No model is refitted for another evaluation capacity.
