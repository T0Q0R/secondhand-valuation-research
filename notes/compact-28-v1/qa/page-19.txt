Discussion and conclusion
The information frontier limits guarantees under indistinguishable reports; observed recovery paths
diagnose a separate failure of fitted predictors. RPL links that diagnosis to learning and lowers
inflation at the tested unit-weight endpoint, without private deployment inputs. Its zero-query
component shows that part of the gain comes from changing the estimator. Synthetic honest-error
and failed-query increases, together with finite response regret, preclude a universal dominance
claim.
Stronger-search interventions restrict the earlier negative feedback result to its weak-search im-
plementation; they do not identify a unique causal bug or establish feedback benefit across sources.
The history cap similarly has a useful fixed-peer guarantee but can worsen joint outcomes and
raises same-record consistency issues. Decisions should therefore use the joint incentive, prediction
and verification outcomes rather than inflation alone.
The cohorts are retrospective advertised-price samples. Concealment, costs and response dy-
namics are modeled, not observed seller behavior; fitting seeds do not sample new training popula-
tions. Shared audit primitives and nonconverged profiles constrain computational and equilibrium
interpretations. An independently operated Mac completed six synthetic/seed-13 training cells:
inputs and tree backbones matched, but residual parameters and outcomes failed the original nu-
merical tolerances. Later diagnostics locate the first recorded difference in output-layer gradients;
unrecorded hidden activations prevent causal attribution to a particular primitive. One-seed direc-
tions agreed, which is not numerical equivalence. An isolated float64 candidate has not replaced
the formal experiments. Actual seller data and independently controlled numerical environments
remain necessary transportability tests.
