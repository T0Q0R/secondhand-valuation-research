7.2
Recovery-path regularization: improvement and costs
RPL compares λ = 0, .1, .5, 1 at β = .5 and λ = 0, .5 at β = 0, across capacities 0, .1, .3 with the
same no-feedback reset schedule. Each branch uses its same-gap zero-path control. The declared
development grid predates formal evaluation; it is not independent preregistration or proof that
λ = 1 was a prespecified headline. Weight .5 matches the existing gap scale; neither .5 nor 1 is
claimed optimal.
Inflation improvement and uncertainty.
At 10% capacity and λ = 1, β = 0.5, ratios of mean
inflation to the matched λ = 0 control fell by 50.41%, 26.58% and 23.81% on synthetic, JUCars and
MUCars. All ten fitting seeds in each source had lower inflation. The absolute paired reductions
were 0.006268, 0.003306 and 0.001573 log units. The exploratory simultaneous bands over 216 met-
ric contrasts were [0.004644, 0.007891], [0.001209, 0.005403] and [0.000624, 0.002522], respectively
(Figure 1). These conditional bands support the within-design direction without certifying finite-
sample coverage or new-population generalization. Smaller weights were less uniform: at λ = 0.1,
only six of ten seeds improved on each real source; at λ = 0.5, nine of ten did so. Appendix D
retains every seed.
Loss-component boundary.
The matched path/gap comparison (Figure 2) distinguishes path

component ablation and three-seed recovery-edge pilot; neither is relabeled as ten-seed evidence.
