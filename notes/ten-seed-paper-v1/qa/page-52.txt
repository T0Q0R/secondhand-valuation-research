K.5
Positive costs change the conditional feedback ranking
We recompute responses under per-hidden-field costs 0.005 and 0.02 in log-quote utility units, and
heterogeneous costs 0.02hi, where a fixed listing hash gives hi ∈[0.5, 1.5). These are stipulated
post-hoc sensitivities, not measured human effort or monetary sanctions. All 108 positive-cost cells
retain the robust initial objective and suppression score, three original fitting seeds, both feedback
settings and both allocators. Four response starts and twenty sweeps are unchanged. Costs enter
report choice; they are not simply subtracted from saved zero-cost outcomes. Independent algebraic
replay and every legal unilateral utility corroborate the returned profiles.
Appendix J shows a ranking reversal on synthetic top-B. Uniform 0.02 and heterogeneous costs
have lower mean inflation with feedback than without it; the heterogeneous direction is positive
for all three seeds, while the uniform 0.02 direction is positive for only one. At heterogeneous cost,
feedback also lowers honest MAE by 4.293 synthetic price units, attacked MAE by 12.198 units
and failed queries by 0.100 percentage points. This is descriptive mean Pareto improvement within
that declared subset, not an uncertainty-qualified or universal dominance result. Failure remains
high in absolute terms (9.4% without and 9.3% with feedback per listing).
Appendix J separates concealment deterrence from the paired feedback effects.
The reversal accompanies strong concealment deterrence. At uniform cost 0.02, synthetic top-
B mean hidden-field counts are bounded by [0.130, 0.165] without feedback and [0.130, 0.181] with
it, versus directly observed zero-cost means 0.996 and 0.957. These bounds retain nonconverged
profiles rather than treating the inflation–net-deviation gap as exact cost. The legal net-deviation
envelope falls by 77.5% and 81.1%, respectively. Failed queries reach 9.47% and 9.40% per listing;
at 10% capacity, at least 94.7% and 94.0% of actual queries fail. Thus the local ranking reversal
coexists with suppressed concealment and predominantly unsuccessful verification; it does not iso-
late improved feedback quality as its cause. Constructively, deterrence moves this subset toward
ordinary valuation: at both 0.02 cost specifications, feedback no longer worsens mean inflation
and lowers mean honest error, attacked error and query failure. This is a conditional descriptive
benefit, not a fully nonstrategic limit: residual concealment remains positive. Appendix G gives
the utility-based bounds.
JUCars and MUCars retain adverse mean inflation directions for both allocators under every
declared cost regime in this subset. Twenty positive-cost seed/cell batches remain nonconverged
and are retained. Therefore the original 36/36 zero-cost direction describes its evaluated regime,
not a cost-invariant property of feedback training. The full three-source cost table is in the evidence
package; no winning cost distribution is substituted for the zero-cost primary experiment.
