E.2
Categorical support-margin imputation
A whole-field adaptation of support-margin imputation (SMI) [9] used all allowed covariates and
four optional-field groups. One deterministic fitting generation per source trained a sixteen-model
linear support-vector regression family on complete-optional-field training rows. Scaling, category
vocabularies and target normalization were training-only. Missing categories required a one-hot
completion including an unknown token. If the reduced sign-consistent model family or its opti-
mization was unavailable, the method used ordinary median/mode imputation. The original SMI
guarantee is not asserted on these fallback rows, and this is not an unchanged oﬀicial reproduction.
An independent categorical-enumeration and scalar-interval auditor checked 94,527 re-
port/domain cases and every final quotation and legal no-query report. Training cohort sizes were
2,947, 2,674 and 3,368 for synthetic, JUCars and MUCars. Across unrestricted, nonnegative and
unit-box completion domains, aggregate outcomes matched ordinary median/mode regression.
Attacked fallback fractions were 75.3%, 87.3% and 65.6%, respectively.
This diagnoses a limit
of the categorical adaptation’s retained-family assumptions; it does not refute the original SMI
theorem. These no-query fits differ in cohort and model class from the matched factorial predictor,
so their higher inflation cannot isolate a benefit of the present acquisition procedure.
E.3
Acquisition-conditioned neighbor control
ACO-1 adapted acquisition-conditioned expected-loss scoring [12] to one-field regression queries
and batch capacity. A training-only neighbor bank estimated loss changes for sixteen observa-
tion patterns; unavailable recorded bank values gave zero replay benefit. Distances used observed
query groups and permitted nonoptional features, with fixed category/numeric weights and stable
ties. It was a clean-room task adaptation, not an unchanged execution of the original sequential
classification method.
The separately corroborated nine-pair study contained 144 cells. The declared main k = 25
used every capacity; fixed k = 10 and k = 50 sensitivities used 10% capacity only.
No win-
ning k was selected. With the frozen robust predictor and k = 25, mean inflation was 0.01081
on synthetic, 0.01118 on JUCars and 0.00716 on MUCars. Full-SAV-minus-ACO differences of
0.00995 and 0.00431 on synthetic and JUCars had conditional intervals [0.00725, 0.01345] and
[0.00291, 0.00588]. On MUCars the point comparison favored SAV by 0.00027, but the interval
crossed zero. Thus a strong predictive acquisition control can outperform the original candidate.
These results strengthen comparator coverage without implying that one-step ACO-1 dominates
the matched factorial policies or reproduces the original method’s guarantee.
