When Does Verification Help?
Recovery-Path Regularization for Strategy-Aware Valuation
Yuchen Tang
Fengqin You*
Xin Pang
School of Computer and Artificial Intelligence

Abstract
Recovering withheld information can increase a seller’s predicted price when the valuation
model is nonmonotone. We study this failure under natural missingness, charged failed queries
and shared verification capacity. A recovery-path decomposition separates information recovery
from its effect on concealment gains. We introduce recovery-path regularization, which penalizes
increases in positive concealment gain along legal recovery edges while retaining public-record
deployment inputs. A restricted information frontier states the limits of verification, rather than
predicting learned-model performance. In a matched ten-seed study at 10% query capacity, the
declared unit-weight endpoint reduces mean inflation by 50.41%, 26.58% and 23.81% on syn-
thetic, JUCars and MUCars relative to same-gap zero-weight controls. All thirty source–seed
comparisons have the same direction. Synthetic honest mean absolute error rises 0.82% and
failed queries rise 1.10 percentage points, so lower inflation does not imply multiobjective dom-
inance. Stronger training-response search also removes some earlier feedback damage, showing
that the negative feedback result depends on implementation. The claim-bearing extensions
complete a four-stage computational audit covering 1,206 distinct cells, including the 540-cell
ten-seed study. Remaining response regret, retrospective cohorts and cross-machine numerical
differences bound the findings. The supported practice is to diagnose recovery paths and eval-
uate incentive gains jointly with prediction and verification costs before deploying feedback or
regularization.
Keywords: strategic missingness; secondhand valuation; budgeted verification; incentive auditing;
factorial evaluation

Introduction
Secondhand valuation must use incomplete seller-provided descriptions while allowing for deliberate
withholding.
A vehicle with no recorded mileage and a vehicle whose mileage was suppressed
can present the same public report. A platform may request additional evidence, but available
verification capacity is finite and a naturally absent field may remain unavailable after a paid
attempt. This study models the resulting interaction between an itemwise price predictor and a
batch-coupled verification policy. The outcome is an advertised price prediction, rather than a
transaction price or a transfer mechanism.
