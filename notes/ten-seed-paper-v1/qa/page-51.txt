K.3
Allocation changes on frozen models
Takeaway. Score-minimax allocation can help with a robust price model, but the same intervention
can worsen inflation with a masking model. It is not a universal replacement for top-B.
This separate experiment retains the original three seeds and frozen price models. It crosses
three sources, two price models, three public acquisition scores and two allocators at 10% capacity:
108 cells and 108,000 outcome rows.
Seller responses are recomputed for each allocator.

separate evaluator reconstructs 1,080 method/batch cells, 1,134,324 legal unilateral deviations,

Appendix J retains all eighteen contrasts. Only robust-benefit and robust-suppression on MU-
Cars meet the inherited heuristic point criteria of at least 10% inflation reduction and at most

The multiobjective results qualify the apparent gains.
MUCars robust-benefit and robust-
suppression also reduce expected attacked MAE by 7.17 and 7.67 source-currency units, but their

Ten top-B response batches fail the convergence criterion (six synthetic masking-benefit and
four synthetic masking-suppression); their maximum unilateral regret is 0.095752. The minimax
responses have zero recorded final regret over the enumerated legal unilateral actions. All batches

reversals cannot be attributed solely to equilibrium allocation quality. The score objective remains a
surrogate and does not prove general incentive optimality. The completed factorial study evaluates
whether training feedback changes these conditional effects.
