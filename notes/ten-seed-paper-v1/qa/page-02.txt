Strategic withholding has established regression and classification treatments [9, 5]. Audits and
partial-verification mechanism design likewise have established incentive guarantees under their
respective action and outcome models
[4, 6]. These results motivate verification, but they do
not imply that an error-reduction acquisition score, a fixed library of query rules, or a finite-
round feedback-training implementation will improve continuous valuation after failed-but-charged
recovery. Ordinary active feature acquisition also targets predictive information rather than this
strategic quotation objective [12].
We study two complementary questions. First, how does a uniform honest-precision requirement
limit the strategic gain that a hard verification budget can remove when natural and strategic
absence are observationally identical? Second, which implemented training and allocation choices
actually account for changes in valuation outcomes?
A finite oracle enforces common decisions for identical public reports. A recovery-path decom-
position separates the initial report, local recovery and capacity competition. A history-dependent
cap provides a restricted unilateral-gain guarantee. A matched factorial experiment separates initial
objective, acquisition score, policy feedback and allocation.
The central contribution is a recovery-path diagnosis linked to a targeted training intervention.
Section 4 delimits guarantees under observationally identical missing reports; its restricted theorem
is not a quantitative prediction for the learned four-field models. Section 5 decomposes the valuation
effect of recovery, gives a conditional history-cap guarantee, and defines recovery-path regularization
(RPL).
The empirical evidence separates initial objective, acquisition score, policy feedback and allo-
cation. The original factorial identifies a weak-search feedback failure. Matched stronger-search
interventions qualify that finding, while competitive ordinary-benefit and acquisition-conditioned
controls prevent a universal claim for the original SAV candidate. The ten-seed RPL extension
then tests the diagnosed recovery failure directly against same-gap zero-weight controls, includ-
ing zero-query outcomes and path/gap removals. Its lower inflation coexists with predictive and
verification costs. The claim-bearing extensions have completed a four-stage computational audit,
with shared model primitives and conditional uncertainty explicitly disclosed. The resulting contri-
bution concerns recovery-aware learning and a reusable comparison protocol, without establishing
universal superiority or actual seller behavior.

Related work
2.1
Strategic withholding and predictive missingness
Zhang et al. studied strategic missingness in regression through support-margin imputation [9].
Krishnaswamy et al. characterized truthful classifiers and constructed predictors resistant to feature
withholding [5]. These works already establish that natural absence and deliberate concealment
can have the same report. We therefore do not claim that continuous prediction or ambiguous
missingness defines a new problem. Our analytical question adds a uniform honest-quote error
constraint and a hard query budget. Removing the incentive by changing predictions may violate
that precision constraint, which is distinct from average mean absolute error.
