Experimental design and corroborating checks
6.1
Cohorts and source boundaries
Takeaway. The evidence concerns advertised-price prediction on fixed retrospective cohorts and
explicitly modeled seller responses.
JUCars-2024 version 2 contains Jordanian listings from 2024 and was deposited in 2026
[1];
MUCars-2024 version 2 contains Moroccan listings from 2024
[2]. Both sources are distributed
under CC BY 4.0. Recorded prices are advertised prices, not completed transaction prices. We eval-
uate native currencies separately. The synthetic market follows the saved generator rather than an
empirically identified transaction model. Before the present caps, the processed training/evaluation
splits contain 4,774/1,374 JUCars records and 39,244/10,672 MUCars records. Identifier hashing
caps training at 5,000 and evaluation at 1,000 records. Synthetic generation uses fixed seeds 73,101
and 73,102 for 6,000/1,500 records before the same caps. Fitting seeds vary training randomness,
not the source cohorts or generator. The hash cap is deterministic and label-independent; all ten
fitting seeds share exactly the same evaluation records and batch membership. Seed variation re-
flects fitting randomness only. The validation sources were inspected during development and are
retrospective, rather than fresh confirmatory tests. The capped cohorts contain ten batches of 100;
these small cluster counts restrict uncertainty claims.
Original advertisement identifiers are unavailable; inherited non-price fingerprints provide du-
plicate control without certifying independent vehicles.
Targets, identifiers, source indices and
declared direct reveal aliases are excluded from predictor inputs. Correlated covariates remain,
so concealment need not remove all information about a field. Naturally absent fields cannot be
concealed by a seller or successfully recovered by a query.
6.2
Matched training and evaluation
Takeaway. The full controlled experiment and four-stage audit are complete; controlled contrasts
do not establish a general method advantage.
The new design crosses two initial training objectives (mask-augmented and robust), three ac-
quisition scores (suppression, benefit and risk), two policy feedback settings, and two allocators
(top-B and score-minimax). The initial objective factor is a new controlled intervention, not a
relabeling of the original SAV and static-robust checkpoints. Each cell uses the same backbone
architecture, six initial residual epochs and six subsequent epochs in two rounds, with matched
record orders and head-refresh schedules. No-policy-feedback fits are shared across their six ac-
quisition/allocation aliases, giving fourteen unique fits for twenty-four cells.
The implemented
sequence is:
1. For each of three outer folds, exclude its records from the complete training loop. Split the
remaining records into estimator and head-guidance sets, using a guidance fraction of 0.3.
Fit the augmented backbone and each six-epoch initial residual objective.
2. Clone each initial model into one no-policy-feedback fit and six score/allocator-specific feed-
back fits. Run two three-epoch robust-update rounds. Refit the guidance head after each
round, retaining identical record orders across the matched cells.
3. At the start of each feedback epoch, compute public acquisition scores and hold them fixed
during that epoch. Within each minibatch, use the current detached quotes to search seller
responses, with two sweeps and one start at 10% capacity. Reallocate the batch for every
