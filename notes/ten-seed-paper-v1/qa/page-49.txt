The adverse feedback count falls from 34 unadjusted intervals to 31, 27 and 20 under these

Feedback does not explain a beneficial effect in this design.
Define a feedback contrast as
no-policy-feedback loss minus policy-feedback loss, holding the initial objective, score and allocator
fixed. For positive expected log-quote inflation, all 36 point estimates at 10% were negative; 34
unadjusted intervals excluded zero in the unfavorable direction. The two intervals crossing zero
corresponded to MUCars with the mask initial objective and suppression acquisition. No feedback
contrast passed the point gate of at least 10% inflation reduction with at most 5% honest-MAE
increase. This is a negative result for the implemented two-round, epoch-refreshed, weak-response-
search training procedure. The strong-search means in Section 7.4 narrow its interpretation; they
do not alter the original counts.

Initial-objective effects depend on the source.
The robust initial objective lowered mean
inflation in all twelve MUCars conditional contrasts. Reductions ranged from 12.43% to 33.40%, all
twelve unadjusted intervals excluded zero favorably, and all twelve passed the point gate. Honest
MAE also decreased in these comparisons. On JUCars, twelve point estimates favored the robust
objective but only two intervals excluded zero favorably. On synthetic data, only four point es-
timates favored it, and six intervals excluded zero in the adverse direction. Thus the controlled
initial-objective factor has a stronger source-dependent signature than a beneficial feedback effect.
These are newly trained matched models, so they do not retrospectively identify the cause of the
original SAV/static-robust comparison.
