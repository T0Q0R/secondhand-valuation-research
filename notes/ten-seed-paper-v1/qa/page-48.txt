Additional historical control analyses
The following analyses retain the full original protocols and numerical evidence summarized in the
main text. Their original fitting-seed and uncertainty scopes are unchanged.
K.1
Matched factorial effects
Takeaway. Under the original weak training-response search, all 36 primary feedback contrasts
increase mean inflation. This documents a failure of that implementation, not a general negative
effect of feedback.
Which conclusion is primary?
The paper’s empirical conclusion is that verification and feed-
back outcomes depend on the learning and response procedures. The 36/36 count, Table 18, and
the controls in Appendix J establish the well-audited original implementation’s failure pattern.
They are not the headline claim that feedback is intrinsically harmful.
The later, four-stage-
checked search intervention (Section 7.4) largely removes the synthetic/JUCars mean damage with
stronger training search, while MUCars remains adverse in those means. Both findings must be
read together; neither identifies weak search as the sole cause nor proves exact-equilibrium benefit.
Table 2 separates completed computational checks from independent scientific confirmation.
Inference scope.
Every source uses ten fitting seeds and ten shared evaluation batches of 100
listings. The same 1,000 listings are reused across seeds and methods; 4.32 million outcome records
are therefore not independent listing observations. We report 1,000 crossed seed–batch bootstrap
draws and their 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. These unadjusted exploratory intervals condition on
the fixed training records. They do not cover a new training population or correct for multiple
comparisons. The primary capacity supplies 174 factor contrasts and 18 additional joint-policy
contrasts, each evaluated on eight metrics. All six capacities and all contrast components remain in
the evidence package. Comparisons away from 10% also measure transfer from the fixed training
capacity.
