Discussion
Takeaway.
The contribution is a recovery-aware intervention supported by matched ten-seed
effects and explicit deployment costs. Verification quality depends on how recovered records enter
the predictor and response procedure, as well as which information is acquired.
The information frontier and learned recovery graph address complementary questions. Obser-
vationally identical missing reports restrict what a budgeted verifier can guarantee, while recovery
paths reveal how a valid query can increase a learned quotation. RPL translates the latter diagnosis
into a loss term and reduces inflation in the tested finite-response design. The effect does not re-
quire new private deployment inputs, but its zero-query component shows that part of the benefit
comes from changing the estimator. Existing predictive acquisition objectives [12] and strategic
withholding models [9, 5] therefore remain relevant comparators, rather than being displaced by
the recovery-path objective.
Search-strength interventions qualify the earlier negative feedback finding. They show that an
adverse weak-search implementation need not characterize all feedback procedures; they do not
isolate one unique causal defect or establish feedback benefit across sources. RPL likewise does not
dominate all objectives: the synthetic predictor pays higher honest error and failed-query costs. The
history cap supplies a restricted fixed-peer guarantee, but its history dependence raises consistency
and appeal issues for equal final records. Deployments should choose among these interventions
using the joint error, failure and incentive outcomes, not inflation alone.
The scientific scope remains finite and retrospective. Seller concealment, costs, query success
and response search are modeled, not observed behavior. The real data are advertised prices from
two cohorts, not verified transactions. Fitting seeds quantify algorithmic variability on fixed pop-
ulations and cannot establish uncertainty over new training populations. Nonconverged response
batches bound equilibrium interpretations. Four-stage computational checks improve traceability
but share loading and prediction primitives. A separate external run completed, yet failed the
original numerical tolerances; this limits cross-machine reproducibility rather than being silently
absorbed into the uncertainty bands. Testing learned recovery effects with actual seller decisions
and independently controlled numerical environments would address these two remaining trans-
portability questions.
9
Conclusion
Information recovery does not by itself ensure better valuation incentives. A bounded information
frontier and observed gain-increasing recovery paths motivate RPL, which lowers primary mean
inflation across all thirty tested source–seed comparisons at the declared unit-weight endpoint.
Honest-error, query-failure and response-convergence costs prevent a universal dominance claim.
Stronger response search also narrows the original negative feedback finding to its implementation.
Together, these results support diagnosing recovery paths before choosing feedback or regularization
and retaining prediction and verification costs in the deployment decision. The findings remain
conditional on the fixed cohorts and modeled finite unilateral responses.
