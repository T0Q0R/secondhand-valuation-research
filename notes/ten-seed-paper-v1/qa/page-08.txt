5.2
History cap and fixed-peer guarantee
Takeaway.
Removing quote increases along the realized recovery path gives a conditional
unilateral-gain guarantee without changing truthful quotes.
For every realized allocation, use
qcap(r, a) = min{q(r, ∅), q(r, a)}.
Apply this operation before averaging over a policy mixture. The underlying public scores, query
budget, model and mixture weights are fixed.
Proposition 2. If truthful queries cannot alter the fixed itemwise quote, then for any fixed peer
profile and any fixed policy mixture the cap preserves the truthful quote and weakly reduces the worst
expected unilateral gain over legal own reports.
Proof. Pointwise qcap(s, a) ≤q(s, a), whereas qcap(0, a) = q(0, a) = qH for every realized action.
Subtracting the same truthful quote, taking expectations and then maximizing over legal s preserves
the inequality. The null deviation yields zero.
The mechanism deliberately uses verification history.
It lies outside the verification-neutral
class in Equation (1), so it cannot be used to claim a violation of that bound. Equal final records
can have different prices under the cap. The guarantee concerns fixed peers and scores; a new
seller-response profile can change allocations and outcomes. Lower quotes need not have lower
absolute pricing error.
The full comparison includes both estimators and all six capacities: 108 cases, 5,671,620 legal
report rows and 2,160,000 entry–method comparisons. A separate auditor reconstructs absolute
quotes rather than reusing the signed-gain cap calculation. All truthful quotes are unchanged and
all fixed-profile envelopes weakly decrease within tolerance 10−10. Risk has zero improvement in
the primary fixed-profile table in Appendix B and is retained as a strong reference. These are
additional conditional diagnostics, not new ten-seed training experiments.
5.3
History dependence and a final-record alternative
The cap can assign different quotes to identical final records because it retains the pre-query
quote. Consequently, final-record consistency cannot be claimed. A platform would need to explain
the history rule, allow correction and review, and evaluate whether verification selection creates
systematic disparities. We have not measured protected-group effects or established a deployment
fairness guarantee.
