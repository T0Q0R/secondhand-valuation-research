A positive interaction can mean less harm.
For synthetic data with the mask objective and
suppression acquisition, the feedback–allocation interaction was D = 0.004917, with an unadjusted
interval [0.002378, 0.007586]. Feedback increased inflation under both allocators: from 0.010871 to
0.018969 under top-B, and from 0.010638 to 0.013818 under minimax. The positive interaction
measures the smaller increase under minimax; it does not show that minimax feedback improved
on its no-feedback control. Across all sources, none of eighteen joint changes from no feedback/top-
B to policy feedback/minimax passed the point gate. The three favorable MUCars joint point
estimates were small and their intervals crossed zero. Inflation, honest error, attacked error and
failed-query effects must therefore be read jointly rather than inferred from the interaction sign.
Mechanism and practical boundary.
The factorial experiment isolates implemented training
and allocation choices, but does not identify a unique cause of its feedback damage. Section 7.4
intervenes on response-search strength and score-refresh frequency; stronger search removes some
damage, while capacity transfer is not separately identified. These computationally checked devel-
opment interventions qualify the negative result without establishing a sole cause. The appropriate
practical comparison is the matched no-feedback control with the same initial objective, score and
capacity. Choosing feedback from a positive interaction alone is unsupported. A risk score is also
not a universal fallback: its feedback-trained cells are among the adverse cases. Performance on a
new cohort requires renewed multiobjective validation; point Pareto flags in the present cohort are
descriptive, not probabilistic dominance guarantees.
Absolute outcomes qualify an inflation-only ranking.
Appendix J holds the robust initial
objective and suppression acquisition fixed. Minimax feedback increased JUCars inflation from
0.010432 to 0.011855 while reducing attacked MAE from 6902.07 to 6899.03 and lowering query
failures. On MUCars, the same feedback contrast increased both inflation and attacked error. Thus
improving prediction error need not reduce the seller’s gain over the model’s own honest quote. Of
the 432 source/capacity/cell settings after aggregating seeds, 37 contained a nonconverged batch;
eight of these were synthetic primary-capacity settings. The maximum recorded remaining regret
was 0.177513 log units. All were retained.

Strong controls and the original candidate comparison
The original SAV study supplies a comparator check rather than a factorial attribution. Its ten-
seed extension covers 30 source–seed pairs, 1,860 cells and 1.86 million repeated outcomes. At
10% capacity, SAV inflation was 0.021175 on synthetic data, 0.014988 on JUCars and 0.007340
on MUCars; the corresponding static-robust values were 0.012084, 0.012082 and 0.006511. The
static-robust-minus-SAV crossed intervals were [−0.012355, −0.006323], [−0.007025, 0.000777] and
[−0.001870, 0.000210], respectively. These comparisons do not support consistent SAV superiority;
intervals including zero also do not establish equivalence.
Appendix C retains all five original
comparators for each source, including strong benefit-head controls and weaker masking/no-gap
variants. These original models must not be substituted for the matched factorial objectives.

ordinary-benefit extension, reference-minus-SAV inflation differences are −0.00998 on synthetic and
