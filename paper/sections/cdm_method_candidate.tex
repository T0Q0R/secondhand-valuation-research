\section{Counterfactual Deviation-Constrained Mixture Verification}
\label{sec:cdm_candidate}

We consider a batch of $n$ listings with a shared capacity of $B$ single-field
queries. A seller may withhold originally available optional attributes but
cannot change recorded values, listing identifiers, or other sellers' reports.
The platform commits to a distribution over complete verification rules before
reports are submitted, and draws one rule after reports are locked. Sellers
know the rules and their probabilities, but not the realized draw. We initially
fix the valuation estimator to isolate verification-policy design.

Let $\Pi=\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_K\}$ be a finite bank of rules. Each rule maps the
public batch to at most $B$ queries, with at most one query per listing.
Eligibility depends on public missingness; the platform does not know whether
a missing value was withheld or originally unavailable. A failed query consumes
a slot. In our recorded-value replay, verification restores an originally
recorded value and does not authenticate that value or perform physical
inspection.

For a unilateral deviation $d=(x_i,h,r_{-i})$, let
$q_{ik}(h;r_{-i})$ denote seller $i$'s final quote under rule $\pi_k$. The
allocation is recomputed for both the deviating and truthful reports; peers'
reports are held fixed, rather than fixing the original query schedule. With
seller utility $u$ and withholding cost $\kappa$, define the signed payoff
\begin{equation}
D_{dk}=u\!\left(q_{ik}(h;r_{-i})\right)
       -u\!\left(q_{ik}(\varnothing;r_{-i})\right)-\kappa|h|.
\label{eq:cdm_signed_deviation}
\end{equation}
The payoff in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cdm_signed_deviation} uses $u(q)=\log(1+q)$ in
our first source-development protocol, consistent with
the estimator's transformed target, and $\kappa=0$. This preference assumption
differs from maximizing expected currency quotation and requires a separate
sensitivity analysis. The seller selects a report before the private draw;
therefore its best response maximizes expected utility, not the expectation of
draw-specific best responses.

Let $E_k$ be the honest currency mean absolute error of rule $k$ on a disjoint
probability-calibration sample, and let $\mathcal{D}$ be a finite calibration
set of types, peer profiles, and legal unilateral deviations.
Equation~\eqref{eq:cdm_finite_calibration} bounds these stored deviations:
\begin{align}
\min_{\alpha,t}\quad &t \nonumber\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_kD_{dk}\leq t,
                   && d\in\mathcal{D},\nonumber\\
&\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_kE_k\leq E_{\mathrm{ref}}+\delta,\nonumber\\
&\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_k=1,\qquad\alpha_k\geq0,\quad t\geq0.
\label{eq:cdm_finite_calibration}
\end{align}
Signed payoffs are averaged before positive truncation. This differs from
averaging the positive part of each rule's payoff. The estimator and rule bank
remain fixed during this optimization.

\paragraph{Finite guarantees.}
Every execution respects the hard capacity because it selects one feasible
rule. If all deviations in $\mathcal{D}$ are evaluated exactly, a feasible
solution bounds their expected unilateral gain by $t$. An exact optimum has
no greater finite-set worst gain than any feasible pure rule in $\Pi$, since
each such rule is a feasible simplex vertex. These statements do not extend
automatically to unseen types, unenumerated peer reports, collusion, strategic
identifier changes, or a global market equilibrium.

We use violated-row generation for the fixed finite rule bank. Each iteration
solves a restricted linear program and separates against every stored payoff
row. Termination is checked against the complete finite matrix. Expanding the
rule bank through learned responses would require a separate approximation
analysis; it is not implemented in this first development protocol. Randomized
auditing and response-oracle optimization are established approaches, so their
use alone does not establish novelty~\cite{jain2011doubleoracle,ebrahimi2026audit}.
The proposed research question concerns
their usefulness for continuous valuation with public withholding, failed
field replay, and shared batch capacity.

\paragraph{An inactive constraint in recorded-value replay.}
With one fixed estimator and honest reports containing all originally available
fields, queries can only fail on originally unavailable fields. Thus all rules
have the same honest quote and $E_k$ is constant. The honest-error cap does not
discriminate among rules in this experiment. We retain and report this property
rather than attributing an empirical benefit to an inactive constraint. A
multi-estimator or physical-reveal extension would have different information
and accuracy tradeoffs and needs its own evidence.

\paragraph{Evaluation distinction.}
For fixed returned reports we report both
$[\mathbb{E}_\pi u(q_i)-u(q_i^{\mathrm{hon}})]_+$ and
$\mathbb{E}_\pi[u(q_i)-u(q_i^{\mathrm{hon}})]_+$. Currency attack error is
$\mathbb{E}_\pi|q_i-p_i|$, where $p_i$ is the advertised price in currency
units; applying the inverse target transform to expected
log quotation does not yield the same quantity. Private simulation draws
select one complete rule per batch and leave seller reports fixed. Unilateral
response search uses multiple starts, detects cycles, and reports remaining
deviation regret rather than asserting a unique or globally optimal response.

\paragraph{First implementation and cohort separation.}
The bank contains twelve complete rules: no acquisition, suppression, ordinary
benefit, before-query risk, four constant-field priorities, and four identifier
hash priorities. All fitted ranking rules receive the same public missingness.
For each fixed parent estimator, the estimator partition is approximately
70\% of training records; the unused 30\% is split equally between fresh
head fitting and mixture-probability calibration. These two roles and the
source evaluation records are disjoint. This first mechanism study does not
cross-fit the complete CDM selection process; it cannot inherit the
parent SAV head's outer-target guarantee merely by reusing a price estimator.

Five calibration peer profiles are retained: honest reports, no-query
price-maximizing reports, a fixed random profile, and reports responding to
suppression or benefit allocation. Every legal single-seller concealment is
enumerated against each fixed profile, and both counterfactual allocations
are recomputed. The first implementation uses a fixed bank and row generation;
it does not learn additional policy columns. Evaluation uses four starts and
twenty response sweeps. Twenty predetermined common simulation uniforms per
batch select complete private rules after the reports are fixed. They do not
allow sellers to respond to a realized draw, and cannot certify unseen-peer
or whole-game incentive compatibility.
