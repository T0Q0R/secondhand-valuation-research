\begin{abstract}
Verification can recover withheld information without reducing a seller's
valuation gain. We study secondhand-price prediction with natural missingness,
charged failed queries and batch capacity. A restricted information frontier
bounds verification under uniform honest precision; recovery-path analysis
instead diagnoses actual learned models. Exhaustive prediction tables show
gain-increasing partial recoveries on all three cohorts.
A ten-seed factorial study separates initial objective, acquisition, feedback
and allocation across 4,320 cells and 4.32 million repeated records. At 10\%
capacity, all 36 zero-cost feedback contrasts worsen inflation; a post-hoc
simultaneous sensitivity retains 31 adverse intervals for that family.
Removing both real-source evaluation caps preserves all 24 adverse directions,
with 22 unadjusted intervals excluding zero. For the two crossing-zero MUCars
contrasts in the uncapped design, fitting-seed main effects explain over 98\%
of conditional bootstrap variance; this neither establishes equivalence nor measures training-population
uncertainty. Ordinary-benefit and acquisition-conditioned controls also outperform
the original SAV candidate on synthetic and JUCars inflation. A history cap
can worsen attacked error or query failures after responses change. Positive
costs reverse a synthetic subset ranking alongside sharply reduced concealment
and high query failure, limiting its interpretation as better feedback.
A restricted final-record envelope illustrates honest-error and coverage costs.
We deliver an explicit audit contract and validator, disclose shared audit
primitives, and add an exhaustive state-transition oracle. The contribution is
a controlled audit, a recovery failure diagnostic and explicit information
and deployment boundaries, without a universal method advantage.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} strategic missingness; secondhand valuation; budgeted verification; incentive auditing; factorial evaluation

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Secondhand valuation must use incomplete seller-provided descriptions while
allowing for deliberate withholding. A vehicle with no recorded mileage and a
vehicle whose mileage was suppressed can present the same public report. A
platform may request additional evidence, but available verification capacity
is finite and a naturally absent field may remain unavailable after a paid
attempt. This study models the resulting interaction between an itemwise price
predictor and a batch-coupled verification policy. The outcome is an advertised
price prediction, rather than a transaction price or a transfer mechanism.

A query need not reduce the incentive created by an incomplete report. Consider
an illustrative nonmonotone predictor whose quotes are 10 for a truthful record,
12 after two fields are withheld, and 14 after only one is recovered. Successful
partial recovery then raises the quote. These illustrative quote units are not
empirical measurements. They expose a distinction between obtaining information
and using the recovered record to reduce a seller's expected gain. Moreover,
changing one report can displace another listing from a hard-capacity query set.
Information value evaluated in isolation does not capture that competition.

Strategic withholding has established regression and classification treatments
~\cite{smi,withheld}. Audits and partial-verification mechanism design likewise
have established incentive guarantees under their respective action and outcome
models ~\cite{audits,partial}. These results motivate verification, but they do
not imply that an error-reduction acquisition score, a fixed library of query
rules, or a finite-round feedback-training implementation will improve
continuous valuation after failed-but-charged recovery. Ordinary active feature
acquisition also targets predictive information rather than this strategic
quotation objective ~\cite{activeoracle}.

We study two complementary questions. First, how does a uniform honest-precision
requirement limit the strategic gain that a hard verification budget can remove
when natural and strategic absence are observationally identical? Second, which
implemented training and allocation choices actually account for changes in
valuation outcomes?

A finite oracle enforces common decisions for identical
public reports. A recovery-path decomposition separates the initial report,
local recovery and capacity competition. A history-dependent cap provides a
restricted unilateral-gain guarantee. A matched factorial experiment separates
initial objective, acquisition score, policy feedback and allocation.

The main contribution is a controlled audit of when verification changes valuation incentives and predictive costs.
Section~\ref{sec:information} uses a restricted precision--incentive frontier to define the information boundary. It does not supply a performance prediction for the learned four-field models. Section~\ref{sec:recovery} identifies a recovery-path failure and states the cap's fixed-peer guarantee. Section~\ref{sec:empirical_recovery} checks that failure in the actual saved model class. Sections~\ref{sec:experiment_setup}
and~\ref{sec:factorial_results} report a fully audited, ten-seed, three-source
factorial study. At its prespecified 10\% capacity, all 36 feedback comparisons
increase mean inflation; a robust initial objective instead helps consistently
on MUCars. Competitive ordinary-benefit and acquisition-conditioned controls have lower inflation than the original SAV candidate on synthetic and JUCars comparisons. Post-hoc simultaneous sensitivity and multiobjective outcomes bound the negative finding. Positive interactions can represent less harm rather than improvement. These contributions concern the stated information boundary and
implementation; they do not claim a new general linear-programming method,
universal method superiority or a market-equilibrium guarantee.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Strategic withholding and predictive missingness}
Zhang et al. studied strategic missingness in regression through support-margin
imputation ~\cite{smi}. Krishnaswamy et al. characterized truthful classifiers
and constructed predictors resistant to feature withholding ~\cite{withheld}.
These works already establish that natural absence and deliberate concealment
can have the same report. We therefore do not claim that continuous prediction
or ambiguous missingness defines a new problem. Our analytical question adds a
uniform honest-quote error constraint and a hard query budget. Removing the
incentive by changing predictions may violate that precision constraint, which
is distinct from average mean absolute error.

\subsection{Verification, randomization and information acquisition}
Estornell et al. minimize incentives through budgeted audits, including threshold
and top-$k$ settings ~\cite{audits}. Zhang et al. study automated classification
mechanisms with partial verification, including randomized decisions and
combinatorial costs ~\cite{partial}. Marginal-probability representations are
established in security-game optimization ~\cite{marginals}. The finite oracle
here specializes those tools to public report classes and continuous quote
recovery, with failed queries charged against capacity. Its complete finite
coverage is a certificate within the declared world universe, not a guarantee
for unseen types. Active feature acquisition addresses budgeted information
selection ~\cite{activeoracle}; its predictive objective differs from the signed
unilateral quotation difference audited here.

\subsection{Strategic feedback and contextual policy classes}
Performative prediction analyzes retraining when deployment changes the data
~\cite{performative}; strategic empirical risk minimization differentiates through
modeled user responses ~\cite{serm}. Contextual games use public side information
to condition decisions ~\cite{contextual}. Joint score and audit design is also
explicit in recent audit-risk work ~\cite{auditrisk}. We do not transfer their
convergence conditions to our nonconvex, finite-round training loop or introduce
randomization as a new technique. Instead, we test a frozen-policy implementation
under matched fitting budgets. Our constructed policy-library separation also
includes a stronger small context-aware comparator that closes the gap. This
prevents a restricted library from being mistaken for evidence that dynamic
weights or an LP solver are intrinsically necessary.

\section{Problem formulation}\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Reports, queries and seller utility}
A batch contains $n=100$ listings. Listing $i$ has a latent recorded feature
vector $x_i$, including four optional action fields: mileage, transmission,
fuel type and body condition. Let $M_i$ be its naturally absent fields. A seller
chooses a concealment set $s_i$ containing at most two available action fields.
Its public report $r_i(s_i)$ replaces fields in $M_i\cup s_i$ with missing values.
Available values cannot be fabricated, changed or recovered from public
identifiers. A missingness indicator does not disclose membership in $M_i$.

The platform observes the whole report batch $r=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$, locks the
reports and chooses a possibly randomized allocation. It may query at most one
publicly missing field per selected listing, with at most $B=\lfloor bn\rfloor$
queries on every realized draw. A query of a concealed recorded field recovers
that value; a query of a naturally absent field fails. Both spend one unit.
A fixed itemwise model produces log quotes before and after the selected query.
Scores and allocations at deployment may use public reports and model outputs,
but not the latent recoverability indicator or the advertised target.

Seller utility is the expected log quote minus concealment cost. Main empirical
costs are zero; all randomization occurs after reports are locked. Evaluation
uses finite response search and explicitly checks every legal single-seller
report against fixed peer reports, reallocating the batch each time. A returned
profile with small remaining regret is a local deviation diagnostic, not a
certificate of a globally selected market equilibrium. Predictions do not set a
transaction price, and buyer preferences and bilateral trade are outside the model.

\subsection{Predictors, public scores and comparator identity}
Suppression-aware valuation (SAV) combines an augmented price predictor,
a suppression acquisition head and simulated query-feedback training. The
original static-robust comparator changes the prediction training objective
without that feedback procedure. Counterfactual deviation-constrained mixture
verification (CDM) instead fixes the price model and calibrates probabilities
over complete budget-feasible query rules. Its calibration objective constrains
stored own-report/peer-profile rows, not all unseen types. The original library
contains twelve rules and five calibration peer-profile families; expanding the
finite library does not itself establish out-of-sample incentive compatibility.
These original candidates provide the context for the matched experiment,
whose newly trained initial-objective factor must not be renamed SAV or static robust.

For training targets, let $q_s$ be a cross-fitted quote under report state $s$,
$q_{s\setminus f}$ the quote after recovering field $f$, $q^H$ the honest quote,
and $y$ the log advertised price. The three target quantities are
\[
 v_{sf}^{\rm sup}=[q_s-q^H]_+-[q_{s\setminus f}-q^H]_+,
 \quad v_{sf}^{\rm ben}=(q_s-y)^2-(q_{s\setminus f}-y)^2,
 \quad v_{sf}^{\rm risk}=(q_s-y)^2.
\]
A common multioutput ExtraTrees head learns these quantities from encoded
public inputs ~\cite{extratrees}. Honest records and targets construct training
labels only; deployment does not directly substitute them into the scores.
Each policy chooses its highest-scoring publicly eligible field and queries
only positive scores. Top-$B$ ranks these per-listing scores, using the declared
stable tie rule. The score-minimax control instead balances positive residual
scores through query marginals. It solves a score surrogate, not the true
unilateral incentive problem.

\subsection{Evaluation quantities}
For one returned report, let $q^H$ be its model's honest log quote, $y$ the
log-transformed advertised price, $q_0$ the no-query quote, $q_1$ the quote
conditional on the selected query, and $p$ its marginal query probability.
Here $y=\log(1+\mathrm{price})$ and $\mu=(1-p)q_0+pq_1$.
The incentive coordinate is $[\mu-q^H]_+$; it compares the model with
itself, rather than with the advertised target. The separate realized-positive
quantity is $(1-p)[q_0-q^H]_++p[q_1-q^H]_+$.
Honest price error is $|\exp(q^H)-\exp(y)|$, and attacked price error is
\[
 (1-p)|\exp(q_0)-\exp(y)|+p|\exp(q_1)-\exp(y)|.
\]
The subtraction of one cancels inside each absolute difference. Taking an
absolute error after averaging the quotes would define a different metric.
Failed-query probability is $p$ times the indicator that the selected field
cannot be recovered; such an attempt still consumes capacity. Signed log bias
is $\mu-y$, while expected squared log error averages the two squared endpoint
errors before aggregation.

\paragraph{Inflation alone admits a degenerate solution.}
A constant-quote model has zero inflation for every report, irrespective of
its honest or attacked price error. Thus zero inflation is not evidence of
useful valuation. We treat inflation, attacked MAE and failed-query probability
as co-primary descriptive outcomes, with honest MAE as the precision coordinate.
The joint outcome vector, continuous paired effects and Pareto trade-offs take
priority over any single ranking. This reporting choice is retrospective;
it is not a preregistered change in the original experiment.

For the unilateral audit, hold peers' returned reports fixed and enumerate
all feasible reports of one seller, reallocating the whole batch for each.
Writing $u(r)$ for that seller's expected log quote minus concealment cost,
the deviation envelope is $[\max_r u(r)-u(r_{\mathrm{honest}})]_+$; remaining
regret instead subtracts the utility of the returned report. Thus a small
regret does not establish a small gain over honesty. Main empirical concealment
costs are zero. All quantities are aggregated over the retained listings;
nonconverged responses remain in the analysis. The finite oracle's constructed
quote units must not be interpreted as empirical log-price effects.

\section{Information limits and finite reference mechanisms}\label{sec:information}
\subsection{Public-information precision frontier}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} A finite complete oracle can distinguish a policy-library
restriction from the best value achievable under the same public information.

Let $o$ be the complete observable batch signature and $A_B(o)$ its feasible
complete nonadaptive allocations, each spending at most $B$ queries. Allocate
probability $z_{oa}$ to action $a$, sharing the same variables across latent
worlds with the same signature. For each legal deviation $d$, let $G_d(z)$ be
the expected deviated quote minus the truthful quote, with the entire batch
reallocated on both sides. The finite oracle solves
\[
 \min_{z,t} t\quad\text{s.t.}\quad G_d(z)\le t\ (\forall d),\quad
 \sum_{a\in A_B(o)}z_{oa}=1,\quad z_{oa}\ge0,\quad t\ge0.
\]
This is exact only for the declared complete finite world/report/action universe.
Its numerical primal/dual certificate uses tolerance $10^{-8}$. It is not a
guarantee over unseen types or general adaptive query trees.

For the frontier, we optimize both verification and the itemwise quote function subject to the precision constraint; a fixed empirical predictor need not attain the bound. Consider a separate alias family with an all-natural-missing world and worlds
in which only listing $i$ has a recoverable low-value field, lowering its target
by $\Delta_i>0$. Withholding it produces the same public batch. Require
verification-neutral quotes (same final record, same quote), and uniform
absolute error at most $\eta$ on all specified honest reports. Set
$a_i=(\Delta_i-2\eta)_+$. The attainable worst positive expected gain is
\begin{equation}
T^*(\eta,B)=\min_{0\le p_i\le1,\ \sum_i p_i\le B}\max_i a_i(1-p_i).
\label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
For the nonzero interior case, $\sum_{i:a_i>0}(1-T^*/a_i)_+=B$.

\paragraph{Proof and boundaries.}
Couple queries by their private random seed on the all-failure transcript.
Let $p_i$ be the probability of querying $i$. Hard capacity gives
$\sum_i p_i\le B$. By verification neutrality and the fixed itemwise quotation rule, unsuccessful queries do not change the quote. Until $i$ is queried, its hidden low-value world has the same
transcript; without recovery, the honest-precision constraint leaves a quote
gap at least $\Delta_i-2\eta$. This proves the lower bound, including adaptive
policies in this single-recoverable family. Choose quote shifts
$e_i=\min(\eta,\Delta_i/2)$ toward one another and marginals
$p_i=(1-T^*/a_i)_+$. Systematic interval sampling realizes these marginals with
at most $B$ queries per draw and attains the bound. This does not follow from
an average MAE bound. It does not imply zero adaptivity benefit for failed-query
counts or multi-field worlds.

\paragraph{What the frontier excludes.}
Let $k_\eta=|\{i:\Delta_i>2\eta\}|$. Equation~\eqref{eq:frontier}
implies $T^*(\eta,B)=0$ if and only if $B\geq k_\eta$: each strictly
positive residual gap needs unit query probability. In particular, at $B=0$
zero gain requires $\eta\geq\frac12\max_i\Delta_i$. For $n$ identical gaps
$\Delta$, the frontier simplifies to
\[
 T^*(\eta,B)=(\Delta-2\eta)_+\left(1-\min\{B/n,1\}\right).
\]
The lower bound follows because some marginal is at most $B/n$; equal
marginals attain it. Thus honest precision, query capacity and strategic gain
cannot be specified independently in this alias family.

This is compatible with existing truthful predictors. Natural-versus-strategic
missingness ambiguity is explicit in ~\cite{withheld}, whose Theorems~1 and~2
give minimum-cut optimality and a MAX-ensemble characterization for binary
classification. A predictor can eliminate withholding gains by changing its
predictions, while violating the uniform error requirement imposed here.
Our lower bound restricts that escape route; it does not contradict truthful
classification or establish impossibility under average MAE alone.
History-dependent quote rules satisfy this frontier only when they also meet
its verification-neutrality and uniform-precision assumptions on the specified
family. An arbitrary history cap cannot be placed inside that mechanism class
without checking these conditions.

A two-mode constructed example at $B=1,\eta=0$ yields $72/17$ for the public
oracle, $72/13$ for the best global mixture of eight fixture rules, and
$8$ for risk ordering. The eight rules are the six priority permutations of
three items, risk ordering by residual leverage $(\Delta_i-2\eta)_+$, and
no query; this fixture library differs from the twelve-rule empirical CDM
library in Section~3.2. These are artificial linear quote units, not empirical
log-price effects. All 32 budget/precision cases passed a separate auditor.

\paragraph{What is tested in the learned-model experiment.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:frontier} applies to the single-recoverable alias family,
verification-neutral quotes and a uniform error constraint. The empirical
model has four fields, can hide two and is evaluated by average MAE, so these
assumptions do not establish its empirical optimum or predict a feedback sign.
We use the frontier as a scope boundary. The recovery analysis instead gives
a finite diagnostic on the actual model class: enumerate legal partial
recoveries and test whether a query raises the quote above both its pre-query
and honest anchors. Section~\ref{sec:empirical_recovery} performs that check.

\subsection{Compact implementability and contextual comparators}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} Item-local utility permits an exact compact oracle;
enlarging a globally mixed priority library need not remove its contextual limitation.

Let $y_{oif}$ be the probability of querying field $f$ of item $i$ in public
report class $o$. Impose
\[
y_{oif}\ge0,\qquad \sum_f y_{oif}\le1,\qquad
\sum_{i,f}y_{oif}\le B,
\]
and zero probability on publicly ineligible fields. Every declared unilateral
deviation has the affine form $G_d(y)=b_d+\sum_{o,i,f}c_{doif}y_{oif}$ when its
realized utility depends only on its own queried field or no query. Coefficients
include both truthful and deviated report classes. Compatible latent worlds
must share the same $y$ variables.

\begin{proposition}[Compact finite oracle]
For item-local utilities, nonadaptive queries, unit query costs and at most one
queried field per item, minimizing $\max\{0,\max_d G_d(y)\}$ over the above
constraints has the same value as the complete-allocation lottery oracle.
Every feasible marginal solution is implementable under a hard budget on every draw.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every complete-allocation lottery induces feasible $y$. Conversely, fix a
public class and set $p_i=\sum_f y_{if}$. Arrange consecutive intervals of lengths
$p_i$. A private uniform $U\in[0,1)$ selects half-open intervals containing some $U+k$,
for integer $k$. Each interval has length at most one and the total length is
at most $B$, so at most $B$ items are selected; item $i$ has selection probability
$p_i$. For $p_i>0$, conditional on selection, draw its field with probability $y_{if}/p_i$.
Thus every field has exactly the prescribed marginal and all affine expected
deviation payoffs are preserved.\end{proof}

With $O$ public classes, $n$ items and $F$ fields, this representation uses at
most $OnF+1$ variables rather than one variable per complete allocation. It does
not remove counterfactual-world or report enumeration, preserve arbitrary joint
event probabilities, or cover adaptive queries and nonunit costs. The history
cap is item-local and therefore admits the same reduction. Marginal formulations
and their implementability limitations are established in security games
~\cite{marginals}; the proposition gives the specific implementation argument
needed for this valuation audit, not a claim to invent marginal optimization.

Independent checks match compact and complete-enumeration values on all 32 original
market fixtures and 24 multi-field affine fixtures. One larger algebraic fixture
has 100 items, 4 fields, 8 public classes, 96 payoff rows and budget 10. Its compact
LP has 3,201 variables, versus 18,662,296,388,649,543,921 complete allocations
per class. All 57 numerical primal/dual certificates pass. The repaired sampler
has 3,200 exactly checked rational field marginals across 111 interval pieces;
16,000 numerical draws respect hard capacity. The large full-enumeration solver
was not run, so this is a representation comparison, not a measured speedup.


The contextual-comparator result is a limitation of rule information, not an
LP-versus-heuristic theorem. In the constructed family, a mixture over all
context-insensitive priority rules has an unbounded capacity-related gap against
context-conditioned allocation. A fixed mixture of only $B+1$ context-aware
rules closes that gap exactly. Appendix~\ref{app:library} gives the construction,
proof and exact witnesses. Both comparators must be retained when interpreting
the finite oracle.

\section{Recovery paths and a conditional history cap}\label{sec:recovery}
\subsection{Signed mechanism decomposition}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} Freezing truthful queries cannot separate recovery
from capacity in this replay model; use a capacity-relaxed public query instead.

For each fixed peer profile and evaluated mixture, retain its saved maximizing
own report $s^*$. Let $q^H$ be the truthful quote, $q_h$ the unqueried reported
quote, $q_L$ the quote after the same rule's highest-scoring eligible query with
capacity relaxed, and $q_A$ the hard-budget quote. Ineligible or nonpositive
scores still cause no query in the relaxed reference. Then
\begin{align*}
E &= q_h-q^H, & R&=\mathbb{E}[q_L]-q_h,\\
C &=\mathbb{E}[q_A]-\mathbb{E}[q_L], & G&=E+R+C.
\end{align*}
All components are signed and evaluated at the same $s^*$. Mixture expectation
precedes report maximization. The relaxed query is a diagnostic, not a feasible
deployment policy. The original truthful-query anchor would give zero recovery:
that query targets a naturally absent field, which remains absent when another
field is withheld. Its remaining term would conflate recovery and capacity.

Across all nine source/seed pairs and both price models at 10\% capacity,
360,000 component rows were reconstructed separately by complete stable
sorting of each hypothetical batch. The audit checked 11,343,240 legal
rule--world allocations and rejected four output mutations. Eighty-one saved
winners differed from freshly computed argmax labels only through floating
ties; the largest payoff gap was $6.94\times10^{-18}$. Original winners were
retained. The initial argmax-identity failure is preserved in a separate run.

In the illustrative 10/12/14 example, $E=2$ and $R=2$. If the item receives its partial query with probability $p$, then $C=-2(1-p)$ and $G=2+2p$. The cap leaves the reported quote at 12 on both branches, reducing this gain to 2 rather than making honesty optimal. Positive $R$ can occur: hiding two fields and recovering one can move a
nonmonotone estimator to an intermediate record with a higher quote. This
motivates the following developmental mechanism experiment. It is not evidence
that all original SAV advantages or failures have been causally explained.

\subsection{History cap and fixed-peer guarantee}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} Removing quote increases along the realized recovery
path gives a conditional unilateral-gain guarantee without changing truthful quotes.

For every realized allocation, use
\begin{equation*}
 q_{\rm cap}(r,a)=\min\{q(r,\varnothing),q(r,a)\}.
\end{equation*}
Apply this operation before averaging over a policy mixture. The underlying
public scores, query budget, model and mixture weights are fixed.

\begin{proposition}
If truthful queries cannot alter the fixed itemwise quote, then for any fixed
peer profile and any fixed policy mixture the cap preserves the truthful quote
and weakly reduces the worst expected unilateral gain over legal own reports.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pointwise $q_{\rm cap}(s,a)\le q(s,a)$, whereas
$q_{\rm cap}(0,a)=q(0,a)=q^H$ for every realized action. Subtracting the same
truthful quote, taking expectations and then maximizing over legal $s$ preserves
the inequality. The null deviation yields zero.\end{proof}

The mechanism deliberately uses verification history. It lies outside the
verification-neutral class in Equation~\eqref{eq:frontier}, so it cannot be used
to claim a violation of that bound. Equal final records can have different
prices under the cap. The guarantee concerns fixed peers and scores; a new
seller-response profile can change allocations and outcomes. Lower quotes need
not have lower absolute pricing error.



The full comparison includes both estimators and all six capacities: 108 cases,
5,671,620 legal report rows and 2,160,000 entry--method comparisons. A separate
auditor reconstructs absolute quotes rather than reusing the signed-gain cap
calculation. All truthful quotes are unchanged and all fixed-profile envelopes
weakly decrease within tolerance $10^{-10}$. Risk has zero improvement in the
primary fixed-profile table in Appendix~\ref{app:fixed_cap} and is retained as a strong reference. These are additional
conditional diagnostics, not new ten-seed training experiments.


\subsection{History dependence and a final-record alternative}
The cap can assign different quotes to identical final records because it
retains the pre-query quote. Consequently, final-record consistency cannot
be claimed. A platform would need to explain the history rule, allow correction
and review, and evaluate whether verification selection creates systematic
disparities. We have not measured protected-group effects or established a
deployment fairness guarantee.

A final-record rule can instead be defined on a declared completion domain
$\mathcal Z$. Let $\mathcal C(r)$ contain its records extending the observed
values of $r$, and define
\[
 q_{\downarrow}(r)=\min_{z\in\mathcal C(r)}q(z).
\]
If a truthful record belongs to the domain, every concealed or partially
recovered report extends to that record, and the completion sets are nested.
Hence $\mathcal C(r^H)\subseteq\mathcal C(r)$ and
$q_{\downarrow}(r)\le q_{\downarrow}(r^H)$. Nonnegative concealment costs
therefore prevent a gain over this rule's own truthful quote, regardless of
which admissible partial query is allocated. The rule uses only the final
record and its fixed domain. This is an order-preserving envelope, not a new
general imputation technique; truthful withholding predictors are established
prior work~\cite{withheld,smi}.

The alternative can lower honest quotes and worsen honest MAE, unlike the
history cap's truthful-quote preservation. Its guarantee needs completion-domain
coverage and nonempty nested sets; unsupported records require abstention or
an explicitly uncertified fallback. A three-seed categorical-domain pilot
quantifies one limited honest-error cost: MUCars shared-support MAE increases
by 10.49--23.87 currency units, concentrated among 28 naturally incomplete
records. Mileage is observed and fixed, so this is not a tested four-field
replacement (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism_diagnostics}).

\section{Experimental design and corroborating checks}\label{sec:experiment_setup}
\subsection{Cohorts and source boundaries}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} The evidence concerns advertised-price prediction
on fixed retrospective cohorts and explicitly modeled seller responses.

JUCars-2024 version 2 contains Jordanian listings from 2024 and was deposited
in 2026 ~\cite{jucarsdata}; MUCars-2024 version 2 contains Moroccan listings
from 2024 ~\cite{mucarsdata}. Both sources are distributed under CC BY 4.0.
Recorded prices are advertised prices, not completed transaction prices.
We evaluate native currencies separately. The synthetic market follows the
saved generator rather than an empirically identified transaction model.
Before the present caps, the processed training/evaluation splits contain
4,774/1,374 JUCars records and 39,244/10,672 MUCars records. Identifier hashing
caps training at 5,000 and evaluation at 1,000 records. Synthetic generation
uses fixed seeds 73,101 and 73,102 for 6,000/1,500 records before the same caps.
Fitting seeds vary training randomness, not the source cohorts or generator.
The hash cap is deterministic and label-independent; all ten fitting seeds share exactly the same evaluation records and batch membership. Seed variation reflects fitting randomness only. The validation sources were inspected during development and are retrospective, rather than fresh confirmatory tests. The capped cohorts contain ten batches of 100; these small cluster counts restrict uncertainty claims.

Original advertisement identifiers are unavailable; inherited non-price
fingerprints provide duplicate control without certifying independent vehicles.
Targets, identifiers, source indices and declared direct reveal aliases are
excluded from predictor inputs. Correlated covariates remain, so concealment
need not remove all information about a field. Naturally absent fields cannot
be concealed by a seller or successfully recovered by a query.

\subsection{Matched training and evaluation}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} The full controlled experiment and four-stage audit are complete;
controlled contrasts do not establish a general method advantage.

The new design crosses two initial training objectives (mask-augmented and
robust), three acquisition scores (suppression, benefit and risk), two policy
feedback settings, and two allocators (top-$B$ and score-minimax). The initial
objective factor is a new controlled intervention, not a relabeling of the
original SAV and static-robust checkpoints. Each cell uses the same backbone
architecture, six initial residual epochs and six subsequent epochs in two
rounds, with matched record orders and head-refresh schedules. No-policy-feedback
fits are shared across their six acquisition/allocation aliases, giving fourteen
unique fits for twenty-four cells. The implemented sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each of three outer folds, exclude its records from the complete
training loop. Split the remaining records into estimator and head-guidance
sets, using a guidance fraction of 0.3. Fit the augmented backbone and each
six-epoch initial residual objective.
\item Clone each initial model into one no-policy-feedback fit and six
score/allocator-specific feedback fits. Run two three-epoch robust-update
rounds. Refit the guidance head after each round, retaining identical record
orders across the matched cells.
\item At the start of each feedback epoch, compute public acquisition scores
and hold them fixed during that epoch. Within each minibatch, use the current
detached quotes to search seller responses, with two sweeps and one start at
10\% capacity. Reallocate the batch for every candidate report. Update only
the residual model using the resulting frozen response and probabilities.
The no-policy-feedback control instead searches valid reports with no queries;
it still receives the same six robust-update epochs.
\item Predict every excluded fold with its corresponding trained model. Fit
the deployment model through the same training sequence, then refit its
160-tree deployment heads using the assembled outer-fold quote targets and
the deployment model's encoded inputs. These heads are used for evaluation;
the deployment residual model is not updated after this final head fit.
\item Evaluate each fixed deployment model at capacities 0, 1, 5, 10, 20 and
30\%, using four response starts and at most twenty sweeps. Compute expected
quotes and errors analytically from the query marginals; retain every final
profile and its independently checked unilateral regret.
\end{enumerate}
This protocol does not refit a separate model for each evaluation capacity.
Effects away from 10\% therefore include transfer from the fixed training
capacity. Evaluation uses stronger response search than training, but finite
search still does not certify a global best-response equilibrium.

For the score-minimax control, an item's highest positive publicly eligible
score is $g_i$. We minimize $\max_i g_i(1-p_i)$ subject to $0\le p_i\le1$ and
$\sum_i p_i\le B$, then realize the marginals with a private systematic draw.
This is a score surrogate and an established resource-allocation construction;
it is not a guarantee about report-dependent unilateral incentives. Every
candidate seller report therefore triggers a fresh batch allocation in the
response calculation.

For a frozen within-step query marginal $p$, training uses the expected
post-query squared error,
\[
 (1-p)(q_0-y)^2+p(q_1-y)^2
 =((1-p)q_0+pq_1-y)^2+p(1-p)(q_1-q_0)^2.
\]
Taking squared error after averaging quotes would omit the variance term.
The gain penalty instead uses the positive part of expected gain, matching
the expected-utility objective. Gradients do not pass through the discrete
report search, field selection or allocation probabilities; this remains a
frozen-policy training surrogate.

Writing $L_H$ for truthful squared error, $L_V$ for mean squared error over
valid report states and $L_A$ for the squared anchor to the backbone's honest
quote, the mask objective is $L_H+0.25L_V+0.25L_A$. The robust objective adds
$0.5L_{\rm attack}+0.5L_{\rm gain}$, where
$L_{\rm gain}=\mathbb{E}[(\mu-q^H-0.02)_+]$ and $L_{\rm attack}$ averages the
endpoint squared errors before expectation. The honest anchor in the gain
penalty is detached. AdamW uses weight decay $10^{-4}$ and gradient clipping
at norm 5. The residual has two width-64 ReLU hidden layers and a scalar output.
The subsequent robust update combines truthful squared error, a quarter-weighted
mean squared error over valid report states, a quarter-weighted anchor to the
backbone's truthful quote, and half-weighted expected attacked error and gain
penalty. The declared weights and twelve-epoch total are common across matched
cells. The distinction between expected squared error and squared expected
quote is necessary for correct stochastic replay training; the variance identity
itself is not a new mathematical result.

For attribution, at fixed source, initial objective, acquisition score and
capacity, let $L_{f,a}$ be an outcome under feedback setting $f$ and allocator
$a$. We report the interaction
\[
 D=(L_{\mathrm{none},\mathrm{minimax}}-L_{\mathrm{policy},\mathrm{minimax}})
  -(L_{\mathrm{none},\mathrm{top}B}-L_{\mathrm{policy},\mathrm{top}B}).
\]
For loss-like outcomes, positive $D$ means feedback has a larger incremental
benefit with minimax. Both constituent effects must also be shown: positive
$D$ alone does not establish that either feedback policy is beneficial. The
fixed analysis contract contains 58 conditional factor contrasts per
source/capacity, including six feedback--allocation interactions. The primary
capacity therefore has 174 exploratory contrasts, all retained rather than
screened for favorable directions. No individual unadjusted interval is treated
as a confirmatory finding across this family.

The full experiment completed thirty source--seed pairs and 4,320 cells.
The training audit reconstructed hash-ranked partitions, matched minibatch
orders and 2,068,360 held-out prediction rows. It refitted all
420 deployment heads, comparing tree structure and leaf values, and rejected
altered partitions, permutations and predictions. This audit verifies fit
provenance and head targets, rather than replaying every optimizer step.
The outcome audit checked 4,320,000 recorded outcomes and 45,372,960 legal
unilateral deviations, including reallocation after each changed report.
A separate statistical checker reconstructed 9,216 metric--contrast rows,
validated contrast semantics and intervals, and rejected altered interaction
signs and interval endpoints. The full four-stage completion receipt was
issued on 13 October 2026.

\paragraph{Audit dependency and independent state coverage.}
The historical outcome audit is a corroborating re-derivation with shared
primitives. It imports the separate replay checker and shares the production
state representation, data loader, prediction and head-scoring APIs. It does
not independently reimplement those APIs or every training-library operation.
Its rejection of changed outputs therefore does not certify absence of a
shared state-transition bug.

A separate bit-mask oracle now checks state membership, validity,
materialized values, public missingness and query success/failure without
using production state or query logic in the oracle. Production StateSpace is
only the system under test. All 16 natural masks and maximum-hidden values
0 through 4 cover 1,188 fixture field transitions. Direct CSV checks on all
1,374 JUCars and 10,672 MUCars validation records cover another 521,164 legal
field transitions. Four injected enumeration/transition/materialization
faults are rejected. Model prediction, scoring, dataset construction and
optimizer libraries remain outside this independent oracle's scope.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Matched factorial effects}\label{sec:factorial_results}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} At the prespecified 10\% capacity, none of the
36 feedback comparisons reduces mean inflation. A robust initial objective
helps consistently on MUCars, but positive interactions do not imply beneficial
feedback or a successful combined policy.

\paragraph{Inference scope.}
Every source uses ten fitting seeds and ten shared evaluation batches of
100 listings. The same 1,000 listings are reused across seeds and methods;
4.32 million outcome records are therefore not independent listing observations.
We report 1,000 crossed seed--batch bootstrap draws and their 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles. These unadjusted exploratory intervals condition on the fixed
training records. They do not cover a new training population or correct for
multiple comparisons. The primary capacity supplies 174 factor contrasts and
18 additional joint-policy contrasts, each evaluated on eight metrics.
All six capacities and all contrast components remain in the evidence package.
Comparisons away from 10\% also measure transfer from the fixed training capacity.

\paragraph{Post-hoc simultaneous sensitivity.}
We supplement the original unadjusted intervals with 20,000 centered crossed
bootstrap draws~\cite{pigeonhole}. The family maximum of absolute standardized
errors supplies one common critical value; scales are the conditional standard
deviations of the crossed bootstrap, not re-estimated inside each draw.
Pairing across methods and metrics is retained. We report the 36 inflation
feedback contrasts, the same contrasts on three co-primary outcomes (108),
and all 1,536 primary contrast/metric combinations. A source-wise Bonferroni
sensitivity avoids requiring cross-source independence. Resampling maxima
are established multiple-testing tools~\cite{romanowolf}; this is a single-step
sensitivity analysis, not a transfer of their finite-sample guarantee.

The adverse feedback count falls from 34 unadjusted intervals to 31, 27 and
20 under these three families (Table~\ref{tab:simultaneous}). Source-wise bands
retain 31, 27 and 19. The original 36 adverse point estimates remain unchanged.
No family certifies that every contrast is harmful. With only ten seeds and
ten batches, approximate simultaneous bands do not establish finite-sample
coverage, fresh-cohort confirmation or training-population uncertainty.

\paragraph{Feedback does not explain a beneficial effect in this design.}
Define a feedback contrast as no-policy-feedback loss minus policy-feedback
loss, holding the initial objective, score and allocator fixed. For positive
expected log-quote inflation, all 36 point estimates at 10\% were negative;
34 unadjusted intervals excluded zero in the unfavorable direction.
The two intervals crossing zero corresponded to MUCars with the mask initial
objective and suppression acquisition. No feedback contrast passed the point
gate of at least 10\% inflation reduction with at most 5\% honest-MAE increase.
This is a negative result for the implemented two-round frozen-policy training
procedure, not an impossibility result for feedback learning.

\paragraph{Initial-objective effects depend on the source.}
The robust initial objective lowered mean inflation in all twelve MUCars
conditional contrasts. Reductions ranged from 12.43\% to 33.40\%, all twelve
unadjusted intervals excluded zero favorably, and all twelve passed the point
gate. Honest MAE also decreased in these comparisons. On JUCars, twelve point
estimates favored the robust objective but only two intervals excluded zero
favorably. On synthetic data, only four point estimates favored it, and six
intervals excluded zero in the adverse direction. Thus the controlled
initial-objective factor has a stronger source-dependent signature than a
beneficial feedback effect. These are newly trained matched models, so they
do not retrospectively identify the cause of the original SAV/static-robust
comparison.

\paragraph{A positive interaction can mean less harm.}
For synthetic data with the mask objective and suppression acquisition, the
feedback--allocation interaction was $D=0.004917$, with an unadjusted interval
$[0.002378,0.007586]$. Feedback increased inflation under both allocators:
from 0.010871 to 0.018969 under top-$B$, and from 0.010638 to 0.013818 under
minimax. The positive interaction measures the smaller increase under minimax;
it does not show that minimax feedback improved on its no-feedback control.
Across all sources, none of eighteen joint changes from no feedback/top-$B$
to policy feedback/minimax passed the point gate. The three favorable MUCars
joint point estimates were small and their intervals crossed zero. Inflation,
honest error, attacked error and failed-query effects must therefore be read
jointly rather than inferred from the interaction sign.

\paragraph{Mechanism and practical boundary.}
The factorial experiment isolates implemented training and allocation choices,
but does not identify why feedback increased inflation. Frozen acquisition
scores, the weaker training response search and capacity transfer are possible
explanations requiring separate interventions. They are not established causes.
The appropriate practical comparison is the matched no-feedback control with
the same initial objective, score and capacity. Choosing feedback from a
positive interaction alone is unsupported. A risk score is also not a universal
fallback: its feedback-trained cells are among the adverse cases. Performance
on a new cohort requires renewed multiobjective validation; point Pareto flags
in the present cohort are descriptive, not probabilistic dominance guarantees.


\paragraph{Absolute outcomes qualify an inflation-only ranking.}
Table~\ref{tab:multiobjective} holds the robust initial objective and suppression
acquisition fixed. Minimax feedback increased JUCars inflation from 0.010432
to 0.011855 while reducing attacked MAE from 6902.07 to 6899.03 and lowering
query failures. On MUCars, the same feedback contrast increased both inflation
and attacked error. Thus improving prediction error need not reduce the
seller's gain over the model's own honest quote. Of the 432 source/capacity/cell
settings after aggregating seeds, 37 contained a nonconverged batch; eight of
these were synthetic primary-capacity settings. The maximum recorded remaining
regret was 0.177513 log units. All were retained.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} The supported contribution is a bounded information
frontier and an audited verification-path mechanism. General superiority is unsupported; the completed factorial experiment
separates source-dependent initial-objective effects from adverse feedback effects.

The detailed claim--evidence map is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:audit_map}.



The information frontier and recovery-path findings answer different questions.
The frontier limits what verification can guarantee under observationally
indistinguishable missingness; the recovery analysis shows how an otherwise
valid query can change the quotation adversely. Together they support auditing
both the information available to an allocator and the quotation after recovery.
The compact formulation supplies an exact finite reference, while the small
context-aware rule library shows why a solver advantage cannot be inferred
from a restricted comparator alone.

For deployment, the history cap is a conditional intervention whose attacked
error and failed-query effects need to be measured alongside inflation. The
source-specific SAV results similarly support local validation against static
robust and simple acquisition controls. They do not justify selecting SAV as
a universal default. The completed factorial experiment identifies initial-objective
effects on MUCars and adverse feedback effects across all three cohorts.
These controlled effects do not establish the cause of the original unmatched
SAV comparison, and response-search behavior remains a possible contributor. Fitting-seed resampling alone cannot establish uncertainty over
new training populations.


The study does not infer actual seller behavior from the advertised-price
cohorts. Concealment sets, utility, query success and response search are
stipulated. Only two real cohorts, four query fields, unit costs and batches
of 100 are evaluated. New market conditions, noisy verification, multi-field
queries, nonunit costs and adaptive interrogation can change the frontier or
the observed ranking. These limits restrict deployment claims rather than
invalidate the explicitly audited finite comparisons.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Budgeted verification has both an information limit and a quotation-path
consequence. Uniform honest precision restricts the concealment gain that can
be removed when natural and strategic absence generate the same report. A
history cap can suppress adverse recovery paths under fixed peers, while
recomputed responses expose attacked-error and failed-query costs. The matched
experiment further shows that a positive feedback--allocation interaction is
insufficient evidence of improvement: all 36 primary feedback comparisons
increase mean inflation at zero concealment cost, even though robust initialization helps on MUCars. A three-seed positive-cost sensitivity reverses the synthetic top-$B$ feedback ranking alongside sharply reduced concealment and high query failure, further limiting generalization. The practical implication is to audit public information, recovery
semantics and every comparison component, retaining negative results and
multiobjective trade-offs. This conclusion is conditional on the implemented
learning and response procedures, not a universal prescription for valuation.

\section{Contextual policy-library witnesses}\label{app:library}
\paragraph{A capacity-related global-mixture gap.}
Consider $n>B\ge1$, a fixed core $C$ of size $B$, and public contexts
$j\in\{B+1,\ldots,n\}$. In context $j$, potential quote gaps are 2 for core
items, 1 for item $j$, and $\epsilon=1/(B+2)$ for every other item. These are
linear toy utility units. Use the honest-exact missingness-alias worlds from
the preceding construction. All items are query-eligible in the all-missing report.

The reference library contains \emph{all} fixed item-priority orders and a risk
rule using these exact gaps, but employs one context-independent mixing vector.
Every priority rule selects a fixed $B$-subset. Risk always selects the core,
because $2>1>\epsilon$, without relying on a cutoff tie. Thus the global mixture
has the same query marginals in every context, whereas a publicly conditioned
mechanism may change them with $j$.

\begin{proposition}[Global-mixture separation]
In this family the optimal worst gains are
\[
 T_{\rm global}=\frac{2(n-B)}{2n-B},\qquad
 T_{\rm context}=\frac{2}{B+2}.
\]
Their ratio is $(B+2)(n-B)/(2n-B)$. Taking $n=B^2+B$ makes the ratio
$B(B+2)/(2B+1)$, which grows without bound with $B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A global mixture with value $t$ must satisfy $2(1-p_i)\le t$ for each core
item and $1-p_i\le t$ for each other item, using the context in which that
item is active. Summing gives $B\ge n-t(B/2+n-B)$ and the stated lower bound.
It is attained with core marginal $1-T_{\rm global}/2$ and other marginal
$1-T_{\rm global}$. These sum to $B$ and are implementable as a lottery over
$B$-subsets, all represented by fixed priorities. In one public context, the
$B$ core items and active item imply $B\ge B+1-t(B/2+1)$. Set core marginal
$(B+1)/(B+2)$, active-item marginal $B/(B+2)$ and all others zero to attain the
bound. The low-gap unqueried items have gain $\epsilon<T_{\rm context}$.
Both constructions use the preceding hard-budget sampling argument.
\end{proof}

We checked rational primal/lower-bound witnesses on 26 predetermined $(n,B)$
cases and 257,274 context--item rows. Sixteen small cases also match independently
constructed complete-subset LPs for both policy classes. At $B=20,n=420$, the
ratio is $440/41\approx10.73$. This is a constructed limitation of global mixing over the specified
context-insensitive rule library; it does not establish that real-source
CDM losses arise from this mechanism or that these bounds are first in the literature.

\paragraph{A stronger small-library comparator.}
The separation concerns the information used by the rules, not fixed mixture
weights alone. Add $B+1$ deterministic context-aware rules: one queries every
core item; rule $i$ replaces core item $i$ with the publicly active item.
Give the first rule weight $2/(B+2)$ and each replacement rule weight $1/(B+2)$.
The weights are fixed across contexts. Every draw uses exactly $B$ queries,
while each core item's marginal is $(B+1)/(B+2)$ and the active item's marginal
is $B/(B+2)$. Thus this small library attains $T_{\rm context}$ without solving
a new LP at deployment. The previous lower bound proves its optimality within
the constructed family. It does not imply an unseen-context guarantee for
learned rules on vehicle records.

An independent exact-arithmetic extension checks all 26 cases, 15,922
context--rule allocations and 257,274 context--item losses. Freezing the active
item or changing the weights to uniform fails the optimum check. Consequently,
this family supports a representation limitation of the original library,
not an intrinsic advantage of dynamically chosen weights or an LP solver.
This stronger comparator must be retained in subsequent algorithm evaluations.

\section{Conditional probability-stability diagnostics}\label{app:stability}
\noindent\textbf{Takeaway.} Randomized allocation admits a conditional
sensitivity bound, but small field margins and scores near zero limit its
applicability. Stability alone does not imply lower strategic inflation.

Fix the positive-score support $I$, the queried field of each item, and budget
$B$. For two score vectors $g,g'$ with all supported scores at least $m>0$, let
$\delta=\|g-g'\|_\infty$. Define the score-minimax value and canonical marginals
\[
 T(g)=\min_{0\leq p\leq1,\,\sum_i p_i\leq B}\max_{i\in I}g_i(1-p_i),
 \qquad p_i(g)=[1-T(g)/g_i]_+.
\]
For $B=0$ use zero marginals; for $B\geq |I|$ use unit marginals on $I$.
Then
\[
 |T(g)-T(g')|\leq\delta,\qquad
 \|p(g)-p(g')\|_\infty\leq\min\{1,2\delta/m\}.
\]
For any fixed feasible allocation, the two objectives differ by at most
$\delta$, which proves the value bound after minimization. The function
$h(t,x)=[1-t/x]_+$ is separately $1/m$-Lipschitz in $t$ and $x$ for
$t\geq0,x\geq m$: on its positive branch, $t/x^2\leq1/x$, and it is
continuous at the branch boundary. Applying the value bound proves the
marginal bound. The active allocation set may change; positive-score support
and queried fields must remain fixed.

If both pre-query and recovered quotes change by at most $\epsilon$, and the
primed recovery span is at most $R$, expected quotes differ by at most
$\epsilon+R\min\{1,2\delta/m\}$. If these conditions hold uniformly over
all own reports at fixed peer reports, costs are unchanged, and truthful
quotes are verification-neutral, the positive unilateral-gain envelope changes
by at most $2\epsilon+R\min\{1,2\delta/m\}$. This is not a guarantee for
adapted peer responses, equilibrium selection, training convergence, or a
fixed realization of the sampling randomness.

Three examples delimit the claim. With two items and $B=1$, swapping scores
$(1+\delta,1)$ and $(1,1+\delta)$ changes the first item's top-$B$ probability
by one, but its minimax probability by $\delta/(2+\delta)$. In contrast,
swapping $(2\delta,\delta)$ and $(\delta,2\delta)$ changes its minimax
probability by $1/3$ even as $\delta$ tends to zero. Finally, one fully queried
item with two fields whose scores swap as in the first example can have a
unit quote jump if the two recovery quotes are zero and one. The score lower
bound and fixed-field condition therefore cannot simply be removed.
An exact-arithmetic checker covers 2,916 score-pair/budget cases, including
510 active-set changes; 108 production/linear-program comparisons agree to
floating-point precision. These finite checks supplement the proof.

We additionally audited all 1,080 saved fixed-profile batch executions at
hypothetical radii $\delta=\alpha\operatorname{median}(g_i:g_i>0)$.
A sufficient certificate requires support preservation and every positive
item's top-two field gap to exceed $2\delta$. Its bound uses
$m=\min_{i\in I}g_i-\delta$. Recovery spans are reconstructed for every
eligible item, including items not queried by a saved top-$B$ allocation.

\subsection{Categorical support-margin imputation}
A whole-field adaptation of support-margin imputation (SMI)~\cite{smi} used
all allowed covariates and four optional-field groups. One deterministic
fitting generation per source trained a sixteen-model linear support-vector
regression family on complete-optional-field training rows. Scaling, category
vocabularies and target normalization were training-only. Missing categories
required a one-hot completion including an unknown token. If the reduced
sign-consistent model family or its optimization was unavailable, the method
used ordinary median/mode imputation. The original SMI guarantee is not
asserted on these fallback rows, and this is not an unchanged official reproduction.

An independent categorical-enumeration and scalar-interval auditor checked
94,527 report/domain cases and every final quotation and legal no-query report.
Training cohort sizes were 2,947, 2,674 and 3,368 for synthetic, JUCars and
MUCars. Across unrestricted, nonnegative and unit-box completion domains,
aggregate outcomes matched ordinary median/mode regression. Attacked fallback
fractions were 75.3\%, 87.3\% and 65.6\%, respectively. This diagnoses a limit
of the categorical adaptation's retained-family assumptions; it does not
refute the original SMI theorem. These no-query fits differ in cohort and
model class from the matched factorial predictor, so their higher inflation
cannot isolate a benefit of the present acquisition procedure.

\subsection{Acquisition-conditioned neighbor control}
ACO-1 adapted acquisition-conditioned expected-loss scoring~\cite{activeoracle}
to one-field regression queries and batch capacity. A training-only neighbor
bank estimated loss changes for sixteen observation patterns; unavailable
recorded bank values gave zero replay benefit. Distances used observed query
groups and permitted nonoptional features, with fixed category/numeric weights
and stable ties. It was a clean-room task adaptation, not an unchanged execution
of the original sequential classification method.

The separately corroborated nine-pair study contained 144 cells. The declared
main $k=25$ used every capacity; fixed $k=10$ and $k=50$ sensitivities used
10\% capacity only. No winning $k$ was selected. With the frozen robust
predictor and $k=25$, mean inflation was 0.01081 on synthetic, 0.01118 on
JUCars and 0.00716 on MUCars. Full-SAV-minus-ACO differences of 0.00995 and
0.00431 on synthetic and JUCars had conditional intervals
$[0.00725,0.01345]$ and $[0.00291,0.00588]$. On MUCars the point comparison
favored SAV by 0.00027, but the interval crossed zero. Thus a strong predictive
acquisition control can outperform the original candidate. These results
strengthen comparator coverage without implying that one-step ACO-1 dominates
the matched factorial policies or reproduces the original method's guarantee.

\section{Conditional uncertainty and mechanism diagnostics}\label{app:mechanism_diagnostics}
\subsection{Exact conditional crossed-bootstrap variance}
Let $Y$ be a paired-difference matrix with $S$ fitting seeds and $K$ batches.
Independent bootstrap seed weights $U$ and record-normalized batch weights
$V$ give $U^\top YV$. Write $m=\mathbb E[V]$, $C=\operatorname{Cov}(V)$,
$r=Ym-\mathbf1\,\operatorname{mean}(Ym)$,
$z=\operatorname{mean}_{s}(Y)$ and
$R=Y-\mathbf1z^\top-r\mathbf1^\top$. The conditional variance decomposes as
\[
 \frac{\|r\|^2}{S^2}+z^\top Cz+
 \frac{1}{S^2}\sum_s R_s^\top C R_s.
\]
These are seed-main, batch-main and interaction terms. Batch-weight moments
are calculated by conditioning on the multinomial count of the single
partial batch, retaining ratio normalization. Exhaustive balanced and
unequally weighted $2\times2$ resampling independently checks the formula.
All 48 capped/full real-source feedback decompositions are retained.
Capped components use ten equal 100-record batches; uncapped MUCars components
use 107 batches normalized by record counts, including a 72-record tail.
Thus the outcome matrix and averaging weights both change. For top-$B$ and
minimax, batch variances fall by factors 5.73 and 8.04 (not 10.7), while
seed-main variances change by $-13.20\%$ and $-9.43\%$. This is not variance
reduction from sample size alone. Holding all 107 batches fixed and changing
only equal-batch to record-count weights changes the seed term by merely
$-0.16\%$ and $+0.11\%$, respectively; the larger change cannot be attributed
to tail weighting alone.

Thirty-seed projections divide seed and interaction terms by three; the
batch-noise-free projection holds the empirical batch distribution fixed.
Neither supplies new training runs, equivalence evidence or population coverage.

\subsection{Concealment identification from saved utility summaries}
For one listing let $C_i=c_i k_i$ be concealment cost, $G_i$ positive expected
quote inflation, $N_i$ the positive best-legal-utility gain over truth, and
$\rho_i$ remaining unilateral regret. Since truthful utility is its honest
quote,
\[
 C_i-\rho_i\le G_i-N_i\le C_i.
\]
Thus $\max(\overline G-\overline N,0)$ bounds mean cost below; adding
$\max_i\rho_i$ and capping by $2\overline c$ bounds it above. Uniform-cost
hidden-field bounds divide by $c$; heterogeneous costs in $[.01,.03)$ give
bounds using .03 and .01, respectively. Dividing by mean heterogeneous cost
would not identify mean hidden fields. Positive-cost states were not saved;
all 20 nonconverged batches remain in the bounds. The inequality passes
768 exhaustive toy utility/returned-state checks. The paired zero-cost
concealment counts use saved states directly. Neither co-occurrence nor this
accounting identity identifies a causal mediation effect.
