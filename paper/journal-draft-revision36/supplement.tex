\begin{abstract}
We investigate
budgeted recorded-value verification against strategic withholding in
advertised used-vehicle valuation. SAV-Val combines a mask-aware predictor,
a learned query score for suppressing positive quote inflation, and
policy-aware residual training. A nested target-generation protocol keeps
outer-held-out outcomes outside their corresponding fitting loops. Query
selection uses publicly missing fields, and failed replay consumes capacity.
The complete three-source, three-seed main study yields source-dependent
results. At 10\% capacity, SAV-Val reduces positive log-quote inflation
by 10.92\% relative to a suppression-output robust predictor without feedback
on MUCars, and by 11.42\% relative to its separately evaluated ordinary-benefit
acquisition counterpart,
but has higher inflation on the synthetic and Jordanian cohorts. Its
suppression output improves its ordinary-benefit counterpart by 14.45\%
on JUCars and 8.45\% on MUCars, with no established synthetic advantage.
We also implement CDM-Val, a distribution over complete verification
rules calibrated against finite unilateral-deviation rows. The audited
three-source results do not establish a consistent advantage
over a simple risk rule. The adapted ACO control and common-report transfer
further limit claims of a consistent advantage. Complete equal-allowance
retraining retains all nine source/size cohorts and source-dependent outcomes.
The results concern stipulated seller responses and advertised prices,
not observed fraud, transaction values or physical inspection.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Information about condition, usage and configuration is central to the
valuation of used goods. Quality uncertainty in such markets has a long
economic history~\cite{akerlof1970lemons}, as do models of voluntary
disclosure~\cite{grossman1981disclosure,milgrom1981news}. A prediction system
adds a concrete disclosure incentive: a seller may omit a recorded attribute
if doing so raises its estimated price. Training an accurate predictor on
ordinary listings does not by itself establish that its quotes remain
accurate after sellers respond to its deployment.

Consider two alternative source states for the same vehicle listing,
holding its identifier, other public inputs and peer reports fixed.
In both, reported mileage is absent. In the first state the seller has
withheld a recorded mileage of 140,000 km; in the second the source never
recorded mileage. The number is illustrative. Before a query, a
platform cannot distinguish these cases from the report alone. A rule that
uses a simulation's concealment flag to select queries would therefore
receive information unavailable in deployment. After a query, a replay
may recover the recorded value in the first case and fail in the second.
Both attempts consume capacity. Figure~\ref{fig:missingness} makes this
information boundary explicit.

Strategic missingness is already studied in regression and
classification~\cite{zhang2020strategic,krishnaswamy2021withheld}. Active
feature acquisition also provides established methods for selecting
information subject to acquisition costs~\cite{janisch2019costly,
shim2018joint,li2021gsm,valancius2024aco}.
Budgeted randomized audits of strategic reports already minimized incentives
to lie in threshold and top-$k$ allocations~\cite{estornell2021audits}.
Partial verification without payments also has established incentive
characterizations~\cite{ceppi2019verification}.
In Estornell et al.'s model, an audit checks an agent's reported type;
a detected lie removes its allocation and can incur a fine. Here a query
attempts to recover one missing recorded field, can fail while consuming
capacity, and changes a continuous quotation without an added penalty.
Ceppi et al. characterize verification sufficient for truthful allocation
without transfers. Our finite rule-bank calibration instead bounds only
stored unilateral payoff rows; it supplies no general truthfulness
characterization. The differences define the experimental model, not a
proof that auditing or its optimization is new.
These literatures leave an
empirical question for the present setting: under a shared batch capacity,
does learning to suppress concealment-induced quote increases improve on
ordinary error-reduction acquisition and a simpler robust predictor?
Capacity makes this question consequential because a change in one
seller's report can alter another seller's chance of receiving a query.

We implement SAV-Val, a mask-aware predictor with a learned query score
and policy-aware residual training. The query target measures a reduction
in the positive quote gap relative to the same listing's honest report.
The honest reference and replay outcomes are used to construct targets
offline; runtime scoring receives only the public report, current quote
and candidate field. Feedback training uses post-query reports produced
by simulated responses to the actual allocation rule. Three outer folds
generate targets without allowing a held-out outcome into the
corresponding fitting and feedback loop.

We also implement counterfactual deviation-constrained mixture verification
(CDM-Val). It fixes the estimator and optimizes a distribution over complete
capacity-feasible verification rules using signed, batch-reallocated
unilateral payoff rows. Reports are locked before one private rule is drawn.
Its exact finite-set calibration guarantee is separated from held-out
performance; the fixed-estimator study does not establish an advantage over a
simple risk rule. Randomization and response-oracle optimization themselves
are established methods~\cite{jain2011doubleoracle}, not novelty claims here.

The contribution being investigated is this specific combination and its
measured value, rather than the invention of strategic learning, auditing,
or acquisition. We provide a reproducible implementation, an explicit
information boundary, independently audited hard capacities and unilateral
deviation checks, and matched controls for the action target and feedback
components. We distinguish a conditional optimization statement about an
additive score from a guarantee about actual price error or seller behavior.

This manuscript is a development draft. The complete three-source,
three-seed main study favors the full model over a simpler no-feedback
control on MUCars and favors that control on synthetic and Jordanian
records. Direct-comparator audits, all nine source/size retraining cohorts
and the ordinary-benefit allowance extension are complete and independently
audited; their outcomes remain source-dependent.
Accordingly, the present evidence
does not support a general performance advantage or disclosure-incentive
claim. The target is recorded advertised price, and seller responses are
stipulated simulations; neither actual transactions nor observed fraud
are inferred from these data.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Strategic disclosure and prediction}
Strategic classification studies prediction rules when people respond to
them~\cite{hardt2016strategic,dong2018preferences}; causal perspectives
separate changes to observed features from changes to underlying
outcomes~\cite{miller2020causal}. Performative prediction studies
deployment-induced distribution changes~\cite{perdomo2020performative}.
These works motivate modeling response, but their different action and
outcome assumptions do not automatically establish a property of our
withholding-and-replay game.

Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2020strategic} address strategically missing data
in regression through support-margin imputation (SMI) using full and
reduced linear support-vector models. This is a direct regression
antecedent. The published formulation and variable-domain assumptions
must be preserved in any comparison; an ordinary SVR with arbitrary
imputation is not SMI. Krishnaswamy et al.~\cite{krishnaswamy2021withheld}
study withheld data for classification. Applying a classification
implementation to continuous price prediction would require an explicitly
identified adaptation. A recent joint prediction-and-audit
preprint~\cite{ebrahimi2026audit} further establishes auditing as an
existing strategic-design mechanism. Its linear acceptance setting,
feature changes and penalties differ from our continuous quotes and
recorded-value replay; a setting difference alone is not evidence of a
new learning contribution.

\subsection{Missingness-aware prediction}
Statistical missing-data mechanisms are established
concepts~\cite{rubin1976missing}. Prediction with missing data also has
theoretical and architectural treatments~\cite{lemorvan2021imputation,
lemorvan2020neumiss}. Our use of missing indicators and imputation is a
practical representation choice. It does not identify whether a particular
blank is naturally missing or strategically withheld. CatBoost provides
the categorical tabular backbone~\cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}; work
revisiting deep tabular models~\cite{gorishniy2021tabular} motivates
retaining a strong tree-based control when assessing a neural residual.

\subsection{Acquisition and robustness}
Costly-feature classification~\cite{janisch2019costly}, joint feature
selection and prediction~\cite{shim2018joint}, generative acquisition
surrogates~\cite{li2021gsm}, dynamic information
selection~\cite{covert2023information}, and acquisition-conditioned
oracles~\cite{valancius2024aco} are acquisition antecedents. Our ordinary
benefit head is a local matched control, not an official reproduction of
any of these systems. The strategic suppression output differs in its
target, while both outputs share the same fitted multi-output
ExtraTrees head~\cite{geurts2006extratrees}. This makes it possible to
isolate the selected score without crediting shared machinery to one
variant alone.

Robust training also requires reporting where the benefit occurs and
whether ordinary prediction is harmed. Group-robust work emphasizes
that aggregate scores can obscure heterogeneous
errors~\cite{sagawa2019groups}. Our completed technical subgroup analysis
uses source missingness patterns, rather than protected-demographic
labels, and cannot be read as a demographic fairness assessment. More
generally, reduced strategic quote inflation is an incomplete objective:
a model can make withholding unprofitable by issuing consistently high
honest quotes. Honest error and signed quote bias must therefore remain
visible alongside the strategic measure.

\section{Problem Formulation}
We study continuous price prediction for advertised used vehicles. A source
record contains covariates $x_i$, their original missingness pattern $m_i^0$,
and a training outcome $y_i=\log(1+p_i)$, where $p_i$ is the recorded advertised
price. This outcome is neither a transaction price nor an independently
measured intrinsic value. A reported listing $z_i(H_i)$ retains the original
missing entries and additionally withholds a set $H_i$ of originally recorded
fields. In the primary protocol, the optional fields are mileage,
transmission, fuel type, and body condition. The feasible report set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_i=\{H\subseteq\mathcal A\setminus m_i^0:\ |H|\leq h\},
\qquad h=2,
\label{eq:feasible_reports}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:feasible_reports}, $\mathcal A$ contains the four optional
fields. The eleven candidate
masks include the empty mask, four singletons and six pairs; masks that
attempt to hide an originally absent value are invalid for that record.
Identifiers and other recorded covariates cannot be altered. This threat
model concerns withholding, rather than fabrication or changes to a vehicle.

The honest report is $z_i(\varnothing)$, which can still contain natural
missingness. The platform observes the reported covariates and their visible
missingness; it does not observe $H_i$, $m_i^0$, unavailable source values or
evaluation outcomes. Thus, the same reported listing must receive the same
pre-query scores whether a missing mileage entry is natural or concealed.
For example, a recorded mileage of 140,000 km that is withheld and an
originally unavailable mileage entry can produce identical public inputs.
The mileage number is illustrative. The distinction becomes visible only
through the stipulated query response, not through a secret eligibility flag.

A query requests one visibly missing field. Let $T_a(z_i(H_i);x_i)$ denote
the replay transition for field $a$. If its source value exists, the query
restores that recorded value; otherwise the transition is the identity.
Both outcomes consume one query slot. This is an oracle replay of available
records, not physical inspection or authentication of seller claims.
Each batch of $n$ listings has capacity $B=\lfloor\rho n\rfloor$, and at most
one field may be queried per listing. There is no borrowing of unused slots
between batches.

For valuation function $f_\theta$ and allocation rule $\pi_\phi$, the
stipulated seller payoff is
\begin{equation}
u_i(H_i,H_{-i})=
f_\theta\!\left(T_{a_i(H_i,H_{-i})}(z_i(H_i);x_i)\right)
-\kappa|H_i|,
\label{eq:seller_payoff}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:seller_payoff} uses the identity transition when no query
is assigned. Sellers know the
deterministic scoring and batch-allocation rule. Their payoff is a simulated
log-quote objective with concealment cost $\kappa$, rather than an inferred
behavioral utility. Capacity couples the sellers because changing one report
can change which listings are queried. Strategic classification and
performative prediction already establish the need to account for responses
to deployed rules~\cite{hardt2016strategic,perdomo2020performative}; we do not
claim that auditing or feedback itself is new.

\section{Method}
SAV-Val combines mask-aware valuation, a suppression-output query policy,
and policy-aware residual training. Its candidate contribution is the
empirical value of this particular combination under shared capacity and
ambiguous missingness. An advantage over the controls, disclosure incentive compatibility,
equilibrium existence and convergence are not consequences of the design;
they require separate evidence or additional assumptions.

\subsection{Mask-aware valuation}
A CatBoost backbone~\cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost} is fitted on the feasible
reported states of each estimator-training record. Every state retains the
record's outcome, and each state has weight $1/|\mathcal H_i|$, so records with
more feasible masks do not receive more total training weight. A two-layer
ReLU residual network with 64 units per hidden layer adjusts the frozen
backbone:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(z)=b(z)+r_\psi(e(z)).
\label{eq:residual_valuation}
\end{equation}
The encoder $e$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_valuation} is fitted using training
covariates only. Numeric features use
median imputation, missing indicators and scaling; categorical features use
one-hot encoding with an explicit missing token. Mileage and supported
range/capacity variables are log-transformed before encoding. Outcomes,
identifiers and declared direct reveal aliases are excluded. Missingness-aware
prediction has established theoretical and architectural
antecedents~\cite{lemorvan2020neumiss,lemorvan2021imputation}; this residual
construction is not claimed to reproduce their guarantees.

\subsection{Suppression-target construction}
For an offline training record and report $z=z_i(H)$, define the positive
quote gap relative to its honest report as
\begin{equation*}
d_i(z)=[f_\theta(z)-f_\theta(z_i(\varnothing))]_+.
\end{equation*}
The suppression target for a visibly missing field is
\begin{equation}
s_{ia}(z)=d_i(z)-d_i(T_a(z;x_i)).
\label{eq:suppression_target}
\end{equation}
The honest-reference and post-query quotes in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:suppression_target} form training targets only.
They are absent from runtime head inputs. A failed replay has zero
suppression target. A successful replay can also have a nonpositive target;
revealing a value need not lower a quote.

For comparison, an ordinary error-benefit target is
\begin{equation}
v_{ia}(z)=(f_\theta(z)-y_i)^2
-(f_\theta(T_a(z;x_i))-y_i)^2.
\label{eq:benefit_target}
\end{equation}
The ordinary error reduction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:benefit_target} supplies a
matched target to the implemented Extra-Trees head~\cite{geurts2006extratrees},
which jointly predicts
three outputs: suppression $s_{ia}$, error benefit $v_{ia}$, and before-query
squared error. Its inputs are the encoded public report, its current quote
and a one-hot candidate field. The primary policy ranks the suppression
output, while the ordinary-benefit counterpart ranks the benefit output.
Therefore, this is a comparison of policy outputs and their induced training
feedback, not a claim that the primary head is trained exclusively on
suppression labels. Query candidates include naturally missing fields; the
head cannot receive the simulator's deliberate-concealment mask.

Active acquisition has previously been learned through reinforcement
learning, generative surrogates, mutual-information objectives, and
nongreedy oracles~\cite{janisch2019costly,li2021gsm,covert2023information,valancius2024aco}.
Our one-query ranking policy is a local shared-capacity design and is not an
official implementation of these sequential acquisition methods.

\subsection{Budget allocation}
For each listing, let $g_{ia}$ be its predicted score for a visibly missing
field. Choose the highest-scoring field, then rank the listings by those
scores. Query at most the first $B$ listings with strictly positive finite
scores. Ties are broken by fixed within-batch order. This procedure solves
the additive surrogate allocation problem in Eq.~\eqref{eq:surrogate_allocation}:
\begin{align}
\max_{q}\quad &\sum_{i,a}q_{ia}g_{ia},\nonumber\\
\text{subject to}\quad &q_{ia}\in\{0,1\},\quad
\sum_a q_{ia}\leq1,\quad\sum_{i,a}q_{ia}\leq B,
\label{eq:surrogate_allocation}
\end{align}
with $q_{ia}=0$ for publicly observed fields. To see this, replace any selected
field by the best field of that listing without decreasing the objective,
then exchange any selected lower-score listing for an unselected
higher-score listing. The result is the top-$B$ positive ranking.
This conditional surrogate optimality does not imply optimal realized price
error, optimal seller incentives, or global cross-batch allocation.

\subsection{Policy-aware feedback}
The residual is trained with honest-report error, mask-averaged error,
attacked-report error, a positive-gap hinge, and an honest-backbone anchor.
Writing $\widehat z_i$ for the report after the simulated allocation,
the implemented objective is Eq.~\eqref{eq:training_loss}:
\begin{align}
\mathcal L={}&\mathbb E_i[(f_\theta(z_i(\varnothing))-y_i)^2]
+0.25\mathcal L_{\rm mask}\nonumber\\
&+0.5\mathbb E_i[(f_\theta(\widehat z_i)-y_i)^2]\nonumber\\
&+\beta\mathbb E_i\left[\left(
f_\theta(\widehat z_i)-\operatorname{sg}(f_\theta(z_i(\varnothing)))
-\tau\right)_+\right]\nonumber\\
&+0.25\mathbb E_i[(f_\theta(z_i(\varnothing))-b(z_i(\varnothing)))^2],
\label{eq:training_loss}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal L_{\rm mask}$ is the per-record mean squared error over its
feasible masks, $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients, $\beta=0.5$, and
$\tau=0.02$ log-price units. The anchor and honest error discourage moving the
reference quote merely to reduce measured inflation. They do not eliminate
that possibility, so signed bias and honest-price error remain necessary
diagnostics.

Within each fitting loop, estimator and feedback-guidance records are
disjoint. The backbone fits estimator records, while the surrogate head fits
guidance records. Two feedback rounds each train the residual for six epochs
and refresh guidance targets. Query scores are refreshed once per epoch and
held fixed within it. Selected report indices and allocation are discrete;
gradients pass through their selected price predictions. This is a surrogate
alternating procedure, not differentiation through an exact bilevel game.
Training uses two seller-response sweeps and one start. Evaluation uses a
stronger independently checked response search.

\subsection{Nested target generation}
Three balanced outer folds are assigned by outcome-independent hashes of
listing identifiers. Each outer teacher fits the \emph{entire} backbone,
surrogate-head and residual-feedback loop on its outer-training records,
using a further 70/30 estimator/guidance split. It predicts every candidate
report on its outer-held-out records without receiving their outcomes.
The collected outer quotes, original training outcomes and replay transitions
then form targets for deployment heads.

The deployment estimator is fitted separately using the same 70/30 inner
split on the full allowed training cohort. The deployment head learns from
the collected outer targets, using public-state encodings from that
deployment estimator. Consequently, outer-held-out outcomes cannot influence
the corresponding teacher's fitted feedback loop. Nevertheless, outer
teachers and the deployment estimator use different fitting sample sizes and
encoders. The procedure gives an honest target-generation boundary, rather
than exact equality between teacher and deployment prediction distributions.

\subsection{Seller-response search and deviation audit}
Evaluation performs coordinate best responses with four starts: honest
reports, individual pre-query quote maximizers, and two seeded feasible
report profiles. At each update, the seller's candidate is ranked against
fixed peer reports under the actual capacity and tie rule. Ties retain the
current report; repeated profiles are detected. The search uses at most
20 sweeps per start and retains the profile with largest aggregate simulated
seller payoff. This selection does not prefer convergence over attack value.

An independent checker then enumerates every feasible unilateral deviation
and reruns the allocator. It verifies the search's remaining deviation regret,
\begin{equation*}
r_i=\max_{H\in\mathcal H_i}u_i(H,H_{-i})-u_i(H_i,H_{-i}),
\end{equation*}
and records the largest unilateral quote at those fixed peer reports.
The checker can be exact for this finite unilateral comparison even when the
joint response search has not converged. Its quote envelope is neither a
jointly attainable batch outcome nor a bound over all peer profiles. Both
remaining regret and nonconverged batches must accompany performance claims.

\section{Counterfactual Deviation-Constrained Mixture Verification}
\label{sec:cdm_candidate}

We consider a batch of $n$ listings with a shared capacity of $B$ single-field
queries. A seller may withhold originally available optional attributes but
cannot change recorded values, listing identifiers, or other sellers' reports.
The platform commits to a distribution over complete verification rules before
reports are submitted, and draws one rule after reports are locked. Sellers
know the rules and their probabilities, but not the realized draw. We initially
fix the valuation estimator to isolate verification-policy design.

Let $\Pi=\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_K\}$ be a finite bank of rules. Each rule maps the
public batch to at most $B$ queries, with at most one query per listing.
Eligibility depends on public missingness; the platform does not know whether
a missing value was withheld or originally unavailable. A failed query consumes
a slot. In our recorded-value replay, verification restores an originally
recorded value and does not authenticate that value or perform physical
inspection.

For a unilateral deviation $d=(x_i,h,r_{-i})$, let
$q_{ik}(h;r_{-i})$ denote seller $i$'s final quote under rule $\pi_k$. The
allocation is recomputed for both the deviating and truthful reports; peers'
reports are held fixed, rather than fixing the original query schedule. With
seller utility $u$ and withholding cost $\kappa$, define the signed payoff
\begin{equation}
D_{dk}=u\!\left(q_{ik}(h;r_{-i})\right)
       -u\!\left(q_{ik}(\varnothing;r_{-i})\right)-\kappa|h|.
\label{eq:cdm_signed_deviation}
\end{equation}
The payoff in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cdm_signed_deviation} uses $u(q)=\log(1+q)$ in
our first source-development protocol, consistent with
the estimator's transformed target, and $\kappa=0$. This preference assumption
differs from maximizing expected currency quotation and requires a separate
sensitivity analysis. The seller selects a report before the private draw;
therefore its best response maximizes expected utility, not the expectation of
draw-specific best responses.

Let $E_k$ be the honest currency mean absolute error of rule $k$ on a disjoint
probability-calibration sample, and let $\mathcal{D}$ be a finite calibration
set of types, peer profiles, and legal unilateral deviations.
Equation~\eqref{eq:cdm_finite_calibration} bounds these stored deviations:
\begin{align}
\min_{\alpha,t}\quad &t \nonumber\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_kD_{dk}\leq t,
                   && d\in\mathcal{D},\nonumber\\
&\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_kE_k\leq E_{\mathrm{ref}}+\delta,\nonumber\\
&\sum_{k=1}^K\alpha_k=1,\qquad\alpha_k\geq0,\quad t\geq0.
\label{eq:cdm_finite_calibration}
\end{align}
Signed payoffs are averaged before positive truncation. This differs from
averaging the positive part of each rule's payoff. The estimator and rule bank
remain fixed during this optimization.

\paragraph{Finite guarantees.}
Every execution respects the hard capacity because it selects one feasible
rule. If all deviations in $\mathcal{D}$ are evaluated exactly, a feasible
solution bounds their expected unilateral gain by $t$. An exact optimum has
no greater finite-set worst gain than any feasible pure rule in $\Pi$, since
each such rule is a feasible simplex vertex. These statements do not extend
automatically to unseen types, unenumerated peer reports, collusion, strategic
identifier changes, or a global market equilibrium.

We use violated-row generation for the fixed finite rule bank. Each iteration
solves a restricted linear program and separates against every stored payoff
row. Termination is checked against the complete finite matrix. Expanding the
rule bank through learned responses would require a separate approximation
analysis; it is not implemented in this first development protocol. Randomized
auditing and response-oracle optimization are established approaches, so their
use alone does not establish novelty~\cite{jain2011doubleoracle,ebrahimi2026audit}.
The proposed research question concerns
their usefulness for continuous valuation with public withholding, failed
field replay, and shared batch capacity.

\paragraph{An inactive constraint in recorded-value replay.}
With one fixed estimator and honest reports containing all originally available
fields, queries can only fail on originally unavailable fields. Thus all rules
have the same honest quote and $E_k$ is constant. The honest-error cap does not
discriminate among rules in this experiment. We retain and report this property
rather than attributing an empirical benefit to an inactive constraint. A
multi-estimator or physical-reveal extension would have different information
and accuracy tradeoffs and needs its own evidence.

\paragraph{Evaluation distinction.}
For fixed returned reports we report both
$[\mathbb{E}_\pi u(q_i)-u(q_i^{\mathrm{hon}})]_+$ and
$\mathbb{E}_\pi[u(q_i)-u(q_i^{\mathrm{hon}})]_+$. Currency attack error is
$\mathbb{E}_\pi|q_i-p_i|$, where $p_i$ is the advertised price in currency
units; applying the inverse target transform to expected
log quotation does not yield the same quantity. Private simulation draws
select one complete rule per batch and leave seller reports fixed. Unilateral
response search uses multiple starts, detects cycles, and reports remaining
deviation regret rather than asserting a unique or globally optimal response.

\paragraph{First implementation and cohort separation.}
The bank contains twelve complete rules: no acquisition, suppression, ordinary
benefit, before-query risk, four constant-field priorities, and four identifier
hash priorities. All fitted ranking rules receive the same public missingness.
For each fixed parent estimator, the estimator partition is approximately
70\% of training records; the unused 30\% is split equally between fresh
head fitting and mixture-probability calibration. These two roles and the
source evaluation records are disjoint. This first mechanism study does not
cross-fit the complete CDM selection process; it cannot inherit the
parent SAV head's outer-target guarantee merely by reusing a price estimator.

Five calibration peer profiles are retained: honest reports, no-query
price-maximizing reports, a fixed random profile, and reports responding to
suppression or benefit allocation. Every legal single-seller concealment is
enumerated against each fixed profile, and both counterfactual allocations
are recomputed. The first implementation uses a fixed bank and row generation;
it does not learn additional policy columns. Evaluation uses four starts and
twenty response sweeps. Twenty predetermined common simulation uniforms per
batch select complete private rules after the reports are fixed. They do not
allow sellers to respond to a realized draw, and cannot certify unseen-peer
or whole-game incentive compatibility.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Data and evidence status}
The experiment uses a stipulated synthetic vehicle market and two public
listing sources: JUCars-2024 version 2~\cite{thalji2026jucars} and
MUCars-2024 version 2~\cite{tabarnoust2024mucars}. JUCars was deposited in
2026 and contains Jordanian observations from 2024; MUCars contains
Moroccan listings from 2024. Their recorded prices are advertised prices.
Source prices are evaluated separately, without pooling native currencies.
The synthetic target follows the saved generator, rather than an inferred
model of real transactions.

The previously processed source splits are reused. The underlying public
validation sources have been inspected during earlier development; they
are retrospective evaluation material, not fresh confirmatory tests.
The source training/evaluation counts before current caps are 4,774/1,374
for JUCars and 39,244/10,672 for MUCars. Deterministic identifier hashing
caps training at 5,000 rows and evaluation at 1,000. The synthetic
generator creates 6,000 training and 1,500 evaluation rows using fixed
generation seeds 73,101 and 73,102, followed by the same 5,000/1,000 caps.
Consequently, training seeds vary fitting randomness, not the evaluation
cohort or synthetic market generation.

Original advertisement identifiers are unavailable, so the inherited
processing uses conservative non-price fingerprints for duplicate control.
This does not certify that every remaining listing is an independent
vehicle. Price, outcomes, identifiers, source-row indices and declared
direct reveal aliases are removed from predictor inputs. Correlated
non-alias covariates remain and can make a concealed field partly
inferable. Original unavailable optional fields remain unavailable to
seller actions and to successful replay.

\subsection{Methods and fitting allowance}
The main matrix contains twelve policy/model combinations. Native and
mask-augmented predictors without acquisition are evaluated at zero
capacity. The common mask-aware backbone is paired with random, fixed-field,
before-query-risk, ordinary-benefit and suppression-output acquisition.
The robust/no-feedback residual model is a stronger simple control using
the suppression output for acquisition. It must not be relabeled as ordinary
benefit acquisition. The separately completed original-contract reference
uses its same multi-output head's ordinary-benefit output; its execution and
audit have separate provenance and contribute 54 additional cells. Full
SAV-Val is compared with a zero-gap-loss variant, an ordinary-benefit-output
variant, and a variant whose training attacker ignores query allocation.
The last variant changes training responses; evaluation still uses each
actual deployed rule. These are component comparisons, not official
reproductions of all prior acquisition papers.

All fitted methods use training seeds 13, 42 and 2,026. The CatBoost
backbone has 250 iterations; the action head has 160 trees. The residual
has two 64-unit ReLU layers. The no-feedback control trains for 12 epochs;
the full method and feedback variants train for two rounds of six epochs
each, also 12 epochs in total, without an additional initial residual fit.
The learning rate is 0.001. The attacked-error,
positive-gap and honest-anchor weights are 0.5, 0.5 and 0.25, respectively,
and the gap tolerance is 0.02 in log-price space. Feedback uses a 10\%
capacity and a shorter one-start, two-sweep response search. No evaluation
outcomes are used for fitting, target generation or hyperparameter
selection within this recorded run. These common fixed settings are not
an equal-budget hyperparameter search. A separate nested, equal-allowance
retraining study follows below; all 972 tuning and 324 ordinary-benefit
evaluation cells are independently audited.

Outer three-fold assignments and inner estimator/guidance assignments
depend on identifier hashes. The inner guidance fraction is 30\%, with
the remaining 70\% used to fit the estimator. Encoders, models and target
generation stay within their respective training roles. The outer-held-out
predictions form deployment-head targets; deployment estimators use the
full allowed training cohort through the same inner split. Model-fit
receipts and prediction hashes make these boundaries auditable.

\subsection{Allocation, responses and metrics}
Evaluation batches contain 100 listings. Capacity fractions are
0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30, giving integer limits of 0, 1, 5, 10,
20 and 30 attempts. At most two recorded optional fields can be withheld
in the primary setting. Concealment cost is zero, and each method is
attacked with 20 coordinate sweeps and four starts. The exact independent
unilateral-deviation audit enumerates all feasible masks for one seller
against the returned peer reports. Remaining regret and cycles are
reported, rather than treating a search termination flag as an
equilibrium certificate. Saved common-attack transfers also distinguish
performance on shared reports from performance under each rule's own
seller responses.

The principal strategic metric is mean positive log-quote inflation,
$[f(z_i^{\mathrm{final}})-f(z_i(\varnothing))]_+$. It compares a model
with itself; it does not measure overvaluation relative to the target.
We also report honest and attacked native-price MAE, RMSLE, signed
log-price bias, query attempts, failed replay fractions, concealment and
deviation-regret measures. The primary component contrast at 10\%
capacity compares full SAV-Val with its ordinary-benefit-output variant.
The stronger robust/no-feedback comparison remains visible even when
that primary contrast favors SAV-Val.

Uncertainty uses 1,000 paired batch bootstrap
resamples~\cite{efron1979bootstrap,cameron2015clusters}. The same listings
and batches are shared across training seeds. We first average their
paired listing contrasts over the completed fitted seeds and then
resample the ten shared batches once. Repeated seed copies are not
independent evaluation clusters. These intervals describe evaluation-batch
variation conditional on the fitted models; they omit training-sample
uncertainty. Training-seed variation is reported separately. With only
ten evaluation batches and retrospective sources, the intervals support
limited development inference.

The implementation uses NumPy, pandas, PyTorch, CatBoost and
scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}. Independent analysis recomputes
price-space metrics, state transitions and capacities from saved listing
outcomes. The original code/configuration bytes, role assignments,
artifact hashes and receipts are archived. Follow-up sensitivity work
with saved models is explicitly separate from the main run and cannot
retroactively become a prespecified confirmatory experiment.

\subsection{Equal Tuning Allowances and Retrained Data-Size Sensitivity}

The fixed-parameter study is supplemented by retraining at caps of 1,000,
2,500, and 5,000 training records; the last cap uses all 4,774 available JUCars
training records. The evaluation membership and ordering remain fixed across
caps, and training subsets follow a label-independent hash order. Each size
includes robust/no-feedback, suppression-feedback SAV, and ordinary-benefit
SAV, both at the fixed gap weight $0.5$ and with a selected weight. All six
capacity fractions are retained rather than selecting the favorable size or
capacity from evaluation outcomes. The robust/no-feedback candidate uses
suppression-output acquisition. A separate 324-cell extension recomputes
ordinary-benefit candidate objectives and choices, refits selected static
estimators when required, and constructs its own outer-prediction targets and
deployable heads. Both studies retain the same three trials and twelve
residual epochs. Only their independently audited completed cohorts are
reported as evidence.

Each method receives the same three gap-weight trials, $\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$,
and the same twelve total residual epochs. This is an equal trial allowance
for the specified parameter, not an exhaustive architecture or hyperparameter
search. At each outer training fold, 20\% of its records form an internal
tuning-validation set using a fixed identifier hash. Candidate estimators and
feedback-guidance heads fit the remaining 80\%, with a nested 70/30 split.
The selection objective is the sum of honest and attacked squared transformed
price loss at the prespecified 10\% capacity. Ties follow grid order.

The selected method is refitted on the complete outer training fold before
predicting the outer-held-out records. Those records cannot enter parameter
selection or the fitted feedback loop. Deployment repeats the training-internal
selection on the allowed full training sample, then refits using its 70/30
split. Each deployable action head uses its corresponding collected outer
predictions. Fold-specific parameter choices and the larger deployment sample
produce a prediction-distribution mismatch, which is reported as a limitation.

Candidate models, validation state predictions, public action scores,
allocations, losses, and all choices are preserved. Shared backbone fitting
time and residual-method fitting time are recorded separately. Measurements on
the shared workstation do not establish controlled hardware speedups. Earlier
source validation outcomes were already observed, so this follow-up is
retrospective development and not a newly confirmatory experiment.

\section{Common-profile deviation generalization}
\label{sec:cdm_deviation_generalization}
This exploratory diagnostic was specified after the original CDM outcomes
were available and before its own tail summaries were opened. It neither
refits the price estimator nor changes the primary positive-inflation
endpoint. All 108 source/seed/price/capacity cases and 1,512 method cells
are retained. For each held-out batch, every method faces the same five
peer-report profiles: truthful, no-query maximizing, fixed-seed random,
suppression-response and benefit-response. These constructions match the
calibration profile types but use held-out listings; they do not cover
all possible peer configurations.

For listing $i$ and peer profile $p$, we evaluate every legal report $h$,
reallocate the complete batch under both $h$ and the truthful report, and
compute the signed log-quote difference under each rule $k$, denoted
$D_{i,p,h,k}$. Given rule probabilities $\alpha$, the envelope is
\begin{equation}
 e_{i,p}(\alpha)=\max\{0,\max_h\sum_k\alpha_kD_{i,p,h,k}\}.
\label{eq:common_profile_envelope}
\end{equation}
The rule expectation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:common_profile_envelope} precedes
report maximization. Listings and profiles
receive equal weight, irrespective of how many reports are legal. We
retain the mean, maximum, 95th percentile and exact upper 5\% probability
mass mean (conditional value at risk, CVaR), using fractional boundary mass
where necessary. Whole-case
tails have descriptive seed means and standard deviations. The intervals
in Table~\ref{tab:cdm_deviation_generalization} apply instead to batch-level
measures: paired seed contrasts are averaged before resampling the ten
shared batches. They are conditional on the three fitted models and are
unadjusted exploratory intervals. ``Chosen pure'' is selected on calibration
rows, never on these held-out summaries. The complete exported contrasts
retain all four references and all six capacities.

With robust pricing at 10\% capacity, CDM reduces batch CVaR relative to
the calibration-selected pure rule by 7.34\% on Synthetic, 2.86\% on JUCars
and 6.81\% on MUCars, with positive conditional intervals. Its batch maxima
also have positive intervals against this reference on Synthetic and MUCars;
the JUCars interval includes zero. These gains do not establish an advantage
over risk: its CVaR intervals against risk include zero on all three robust
cohorts, and mean envelopes are higher on Synthetic and MUCars. With
mask-aware pricing on MUCars, mean, 95th-percentile and CVaR envelopes are
higher than risk, with intervals excluding zero. Thus finite-profile mixing
has a bounded effect against its training-selected pure reference, while
the original absence of a consistent risk-rule advantage remains.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\subsection{What the current comparison establishes}
The completed evidence demonstrates that a method can improve on a basic
masked predictor and still lose to a simpler robust control in two sources,
while improving on that robust control in a third. It does not establish
why the feedback loop's effect differs by source. Approximate training
responses, target distribution changes, and fixed hyperparameters are
possible explanations to investigate, not experimentally confirmed
causes. The current full design should therefore remain a candidate
pending prospective source evaluation and final evidence review; completed
source/size retraining does not establish a general gain.

Positive quote inflation alone also has a structural limitation. For
any reported record, taking the maximum raw quote over all further
withholding patterns produces a disclosure-monotone quote: additional
withholding shrinks that set, so it cannot raise the maximum. This can
eliminate measured positive inflation while raising honest quotes and
honest error. The completed generic envelope control exposes that
trade-off in Table~\ref{tab:closure_tradeoffs}. It is not claimed as a
novel algorithm or a prior strategic-classification implementation.

\subsection{Limits of source and behavioral evidence}
The public outcomes are asking prices. A model that predicts them
accurately may reproduce sellers' pricing preferences rather than
estimate realized sale value. Source records do not provide observed
concealment labels, query outcomes from physical inspections, or
measured seller utilities. Restoring a recorded field is an artificial
replay; original values may themselves be inaccurate. The stipulated
attacker cannot fabricate values, change the listing identifier, choose
its peers or manipulate other recorded features. Results do not extend
automatically to those behaviors or to other second-hand product classes.

The processed public validation cohorts were used during historical
development. Their retrospective status remains even if current fits
exclude evaluation labels. Capped samples and ten batches per source
also restrict representativeness and uncertainty estimation. The
training/evaluation duplicate control lacks original advertisement
identifiers. These limitations call for prospective independent data
and real query costs before marketplace deployment.

\subsection{Comparator scope and completed retraining}
Equation-level and full-field SMI adaptations are independently audited,
with explicit domains, discrete groups and fallback boundaries. The full
ACO-1 adaptation, saved-model sensitivities, technical strata, common-report
transfer and public inference diagnostic are also independently checked.
Ordinary acquisition controls remain separate from these declared
adaptations; none is an official unchanged classification reproduction.
Equal-allowance tuning and size retraining cover all 972 evaluation cells.
The completed fixed-setting ordinary-benefit robust reference and its separate
allowance extension are reported explicitly. Benefit-specific selection and
refitting cover all 324 cells, with all 27 cases and 54 deployment heads
independently checked. The completed comparisons remain heterogeneous.
Prospective source evaluation, real query costs and observed seller utility
are outside the available evidence. Nested target generation adds offline
cost that must be weighed against any measured acquisition gain.

\section{Provisional Conclusion}
We implement and audit a budgeted verification protocol with publicly
observable query eligibility, capacity-consuming failed replay, nested
target generation and seller responses to the deployed allocation.
The complete main study favors a simpler no-feedback control on strategic
quote inflation in two sources and favors SAV-Val on MUCars;
attacked-error differences also depend on the source. The full algorithm's
general empirical increment remains unresolved. The follow-up evidence
does not support a universal advantage, deployment
recommendation or journal-acceptance claim.

\section{Data and Code Availability: Development Status}
The reused public inputs are JUCars-2024 version 2 and MUCars-2024 version 2,
available at their versioned dataset DOIs~\cite{thalji2026jucars,tabarnoust2024mucars}
under CC BY 4.0. Synthetic records are generated by the supplied

input receipts, independent auditors, behavior tests and manuscript sources.

\texttt{requirements-lock.txt} and \texttt{REPRODUCING.md}. The complete fitted
models, per-listing outcomes, calibration matrices, query allocations and
analysis collections are retained in local study directories; they are not
included in that lightweight archive. No public repository identifier or
software-release licence has been assigned to the new code and outcomes.
The final availability statement must match the completed, actually attached
or deposited evidence collection.

A separate local completed-component archive contains the named finished
studies' saved models, per-listing outcomes, calibration and analysis files,
plus the four checksum-bound processed training/validation inputs. Its
manifest records every file and materializes the nine CDM source/seed links.
The earlier completed-component archive excludes the size/tuning and
ordinary-benefit allowance extensions. A new local completed-experimental
evidence archive includes both full independently audited matrices, saved

and 162 plotted rows are identical. Original-source/data IO rejection probes
pass; these Python guards are not an operating-system sandbox. This saved
evidence replay does not repeat the original full training/OOF/head-refit
audits or supply prospective market evidence. Neither collection is a
public deposit.
