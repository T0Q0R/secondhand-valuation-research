\subsection{Sources, reports and query information}
\label{sec:source_query}
We use a stipulated synthetic generator and public JUCars-2024 version 2
and MUCars-2024 version 2 listing records~\cite{thalji2026jucars,tabarnoust2024mucars}.
The inherited training/validation counts are 4,774/1,374 and 39,244/10,672.
Label-independent hash caps give training counts of 4,774 and 5,000 and
1,000 evaluation listings per source. The generator creates 6,000/1,500
records with seeds 73,101/73,102, capped at 5,000/1,000. Training seeds
13, 42 and 2,026 vary fitting, while evaluation membership remains fixed.
Native price units are retained separately. Earlier inspection of source
validation data makes this retrospective development. Original advertisement
identifiers are unavailable; conservative non-price duplicate fingerprints
do not certify independent vehicles. Outcomes, identifiers, row indices and
declared direct reveal aliases are excluded from prediction covariates.

The target is $y_i=\log(1+p_i)$ for advertised price $p_i$. An honest report
retains natural missingness. A seller can additionally withhold up to two
originally recorded fields among mileage, transmission, fuel and body condition,
giving at most eleven legal report states. Other covariates and identifiers
cannot change. The platform observes public reports and missingness, without
knowing the deliberate concealment mask. A query requests one visibly missing
field and restores its recorded value if available; otherwise it changes
nothing. Both cases consume a slot. This replay does not authenticate a
record or inspect a vehicle. Batches have 100 listings, with at most one
attempt per listing and hard limits $B=\lfloor100\rho\rfloor$ for
$\rho\in\{0,.01,.05,.10,.20,.30\}$. Capacity is never borrowed across batches.

For the illustrative mileage example in Fig.~\ref{fig:missingness}, both
public reports have the same blank mileage field and therefore the same
pre-query scores. If selected, each spends one slot. The stipulated recorded
state restores 140,000 km, while the unavailable state remains blank.
That numerical mileage illustrates a transition and is not a selected source
observation. The original-missingness strata test the failed branch on actual
recorded cohorts in the Results.

\subsection{SAV fitting and allocation}
\label{sec:sav_fitting}
A 250-iteration CatBoost backbone $b$ fits all legal reported states,
weighted so each source record has equal total weight. A frozen-backbone
residual $r$ has two 64-unit rectified-linear layers and a learning rate of .001. Its public
encoding uses numerical imputation, scaling and missing indicators, and
categorical one-hot encoding with missing tokens. Specified positive-valued
numerical features are log-transformed. With training-fitted encoder $e$,
the log quotation is $f(z)=b(z)+r(e(z))$. For reported state $z_i(H)$,
where $H$ is its set of additionally withheld fields, define the targets
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sav_targets}:
\begin{align}
 d_i(z)&=[f(z)-f(z_i(\varnothing))]_+,\nonumber\\
 s_{ia}(z)&=d_i(z)-d_i(T_a z),\nonumber\\
 v_{ia}(z)&=(f(z)-y_i)^2-(f(T_a z)-y_i)^2.
 \label{eq:sav_targets}
\end{align}
Here $T_a$ is the recorded-value replay, and $[\cdot]_+$ is positive
truncation. A 160-tree Extra-Trees head jointly predicts suppression $s$,
ordinary error benefit $v$ and before-query squared error from encoded public
reports, current quotes and candidate-field identity. Honest references,
outcomes and post-query values form offline targets only. Suppression and
ordinary-benefit policies rank their respective output. Each listing offers
its highest scoring visibly missing field; the allocator chooses the top
$B$ strictly positive finite scores, breaking ties by fixed batch order.
Naturally absent fields remain eligible, and failed replay has zero target
benefit. Top-$B$ is optimal for this additive surrogate, rather than for
realized market incentives.

Write $L_h,L_m,L_a$ for honest, per-record mask-average and attacked
squared log-price losses, and $\widehat z$ for the attacked report after
query replay. Equation~\eqref{eq:sav_training_loss} trains the residual:
\begin{align}
 L={}&L_h+.25L_m+.5L_a\nonumber\\
 &+\beta\mathbb E[(f(\widehat z)-
 \operatorname{sg}(f(z^{\rm honest}))-.02)_+]\nonumber\\
 &+.25\mathbb E[(f(z^{\rm honest})-b(z^{\rm honest}))^2],
 \label{eq:sav_training_loss}
\end{align}
where $\beta=.5$ and $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients.
The static control uses twelve epochs. Feedback methods use two rounds of
six epochs, refreshing guidance targets between rounds and query scores
once per epoch. Training responses use one start, two sweeps and 10\%
capacity. Discrete report/allocation choices are held fixed for residual
gradients; this is a surrogate alternating procedure.

Three outer folds enclose the entire estimator/head/feedback loop. Within
each outer training cohort, a hash-based 70/30 estimator/guidance split
fits the backbone and feedback head. Entire outer-held-out report states
are predicted without their outcomes entering that loop. Collected outer
quotes, training outcomes and replay transitions form deployment-head targets.
The separately fitted deployment estimator uses a 70/30 split on the full
allowed training cohort. Its sample size and encoding differ from the
teachers; this boundary does not ensure identical teacher/deployment
prediction distributions. Receipt hashes preserve all role assignments.
The static robust control in the main matrix uses suppression acquisition;
its separately evaluated ordinary-benefit counterpart is the original-contract
reference. Four ablations and the main simple controls remain in the supplement.

\subsection{CDM commitment and finite calibration}
\label{sec:cdm_commitment}
CDM fixes an estimator and commits to probabilities $\alpha$ over twelve
complete hard-cap rules: none, suppression, benefit, risk, four constant-field
and four identifier-hash priorities. Risk ranks predicted before-query error
and uses a training-selected benefit field. Sellers know $\alpha$ and lock
reports before one private whole-batch draw; they cannot respond to its
realization. The parent estimator uses about 70\% of training records; the
unused 30\% is equally divided between fresh head fitting and probability
calibration. This fixed-estimator study does not cross-fit the complete CDM
selection process. Log-quote utility with zero concealment cost is stipulated.

For each calibration type, legal report and fixed peer profile, $D_{dk}$ is
the signed utility difference between deviation and honest report under
rule $k$. Both complete batch allocations are recomputed. Five profile
constructions are honest, no-query maximizing, fixed-seed random, suppression
response and benefit response. Equation~\eqref{eq:cdm_program} minimizes
a nonnegative bound $t$ on these finite signed deviations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \min_{\alpha,t}\quad&t,\\
 D\alpha&\leq t\mathbf1,\qquad\mathbf1^\top\alpha=1,\quad\alpha\geq0,\quad t\geq0,\\
 E^\top\alpha&\leq1.01E_{\rm ref}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:cdm_program}
\end{equation}
The error vector $E$ records honest currency MAE, with $E_{\rm ref}$ the
reference rule's value. It is constant across rules
under this replay, so its cap is inactive. Violated-row generation separates
against every stored row and is checked against an independent full-matrix
LP. Feasible mixtures bound only these stored expected deviations and are
no worse than feasible pure vertices for the finite maximum objective.
This gives no unseen-type, unseen-peer or global-equilibrium guarantee.
Expected utility precedes report maximization and positive truncation.
Twenty predetermined common simulation uniforms per batch draw whole rules
after reports are fixed; expected currency MAE is evaluated per rule before
averaging, rather than inverse-transforming an expected log quote.

\subsection{Responses, endpoints and follow-up analysis}
Evaluation uses four starts and at most twenty coordinate-response sweeps:
honest, pre-query maximizing and two seeded legal profiles. Each candidate
reallocates against fixed peer reports. Ties retain the current state;
cycles are detected. The highest aggregate payoff profile is retained,
without asserting equilibrium. An independent enumeration checks every
remaining unilateral deviation. The principal outcome is mean positive
log-quote inflation relative to each model's own honest quote; it does not
measure error against the advertised price. Honest and attacked native-price
MAE, root mean squared log error, bias, attempted/failed queries, withholding and residual regret
remain available. Shared-report transfer separates quotation differences
from method-specific responses.

The primary capacity is 10\%. The original criterion compares SAV with
static robust/ordinary benefit; the component criterion compares SAV with
its own benefit-output variant. Their identities are preserved. Conditional
95\% intervals use 1,000 paired batch bootstrap samples, first averaging
matched seed contrasts and then resampling ten shared batches. They omit
training-sample uncertainty and are unadjusted; seed standard deviations
are reported separately~\cite{efron1979bootstrap,cameron2015clusters}.

The equal-allowance follow-up retrains caps of 1,000, 2,500 and 5,000
(4,774 on JUCars), with fixed evaluation membership. Each method receives
three $\beta$ trials, $.25,.5,1$, and twelve residual epochs. Internal
80/20 tuning splits and candidate 70/30 fitting/guidance splits stay inside
each outer fold. Honest plus attacked squared log loss at 10\% selects
each method's weight; ties follow grid order. Selected models are refitted
before outer prediction; deployment repeats its own selection/refit.
Ordinary-benefit references have their own objectives, choices, outer targets
and deployable heads. Reuse of acquisition-independent static fits is verified
against separate fits. The complete 972-cell and 324-cell matrices each
contain 27 source/size/seed cases and 108 fitting stages. All cases are
independently audited; the ordinary-benefit audit independently refits 54
deployment heads. Every scheduled cohort is retained without selecting
favorable outcomes.

The post-hoc common-profile diagnostic evaluates all legal held-out deviations
on five shared profile constructions, without fitting or retuning. For each
listing/profile, $e=\max(0,\max_h\sum_k\alpha_kD_{h,k})$. Equal-weight means,
maxima, 95th percentiles and exact upper 5\% mass means retain fractional
boundary mass. Whole-case summaries are descriptive across seeds; intervals
apply to batch-level measures only. The selected pure reference is chosen
on calibration records. Sensitivities, adapted comparators and workstation
resource measurements are documented in full in the supplement. Independent
audit code rebuilds prices, eligibility, allocations, deviations and outcomes
from saved artifacts; the implementation uses NumPy, pandas, PyTorch,
CatBoost and scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}.
