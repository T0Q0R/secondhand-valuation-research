\begin{abstract}
We investigate
budgeted recorded-value verification against strategic withholding in
advertised used-vehicle valuation. SAV-Val combines a mask-aware predictor,
a learned query score for suppressing positive quote inflation, and
policy-aware residual training. A nested target-generation protocol keeps
outer-held-out outcomes outside their corresponding fitting loops. Query
selection uses publicly missing fields, and failed replay consumes capacity.
The complete three-source, three-seed main study yields source-dependent
results. At 10\% capacity, SAV-Val reduces positive log-quote inflation
by 10.92\% relative to a suppression-output robust predictor without feedback
on MUCars, and by 11.42\% relative to its separately evaluated ordinary-benefit
acquisition counterpart,
but has higher inflation on the synthetic and Jordanian cohorts. Its
suppression output improves its ordinary-benefit counterpart by 14.45\%
on JUCars and 8.45\% on MUCars, with no established synthetic advantage.
We also implement CDM-Val, a distribution over complete verification
rules calibrated against finite unilateral-deviation rows. The audited
three-source results do not establish a consistent advantage
over a simple risk rule. The adapted ACO control and common-report transfer
further limit claims of a consistent advantage. Complete equal-allowance
retraining retains all nine source/size cohorts and source-dependent outcomes.
The results concern stipulated seller responses and advertised prices,
not observed fraud, transaction values or physical inspection.
\end{abstract}
